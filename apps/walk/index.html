Keep going. The fidelity is there.

You asked whether this could ever look the way you want. It can. What held it back was not effort and not the data. It was where the detail lived and what was drawing it. The station above was built in an afternoon from numbers the project already had, laid block by block, and lit by a path tracer instead of a game renderer.

Then and now, from the same spot

Drag across each frame. The present day is laid from blocks: Khufu's 201 courses, Khafre's surviving cap with its ragged lower edge, Menkaure's band of red granite casing. The as-built plateau is dressed casing, satin rather than mirror, sharp at every arris. Both come from the same model, switched at render time.

What was holding it back

scale

Detail at the survey's scale, none at the eye's

The old scene modelled a pyramid as five faces and a temple as its footprint pushed up into a box. The eye reads Giza at the scale of blocks: 201 courses on Khufu, a ragged skyline where the corners were robbed, rubble at every foot, sand in every step. None of that existed, so no amount of lighting could make it look real.

light

A game renderer

The web viewer drew everything in real time, which means no true bounce light. At Giza the bright sand lights the pyramids from below, and without it every face reads flat. Much of the last week went into frame rates rather than into the stone.

weight

Proof paperwork on every stone

Every visual element had to be a sourced record, a TypeScript builder, a Python mirror and a test that the two agree. That is right for a claim and far too heavy for a pebble.

What changes

Blender's path tracer draws it

Light is computed, not approximated: bounce light off the sand, the soft shadow of the whole sky, the sun reddened by the air it crosses.

Everything at the scale you see it

Pyramids course by course from measured course heights where they exist. Blocks sized, weathered and missing by rule, and labelled as reconstruction.

A walkthrough of stations

Each station is a 360° path-traced panorama like the one at the top, in each era, with the slider between them. Flights between stations are pre-rendered film.

The claims draw on top

Every station's camera is known exactly, so the claims engine and the star calculations can draw their lines and stars over the panorama live, registered to the pixel.

What stays: the measurement database and its sources, the sky engine with its precession and star catalogue, the claims engine and the Claude claims runner, the rule that a reconstruction is always labelled, and the current web viewer, kept as the survey tool rather than the showcase.

The plan

  1. The generator

    Today's spike becomes the project's pipeline: terrain, pyramids, mastabas, rubble, city, sky, and stations described as data. The render queue runs as detached jobs so no session is woken by a finished frame.

  2. The rest of the plateau

    Khafre's valley and mortuary temples in red granite, the causeways, the Sphinx in its enclosure, the boat pits and enclosure walls. Today's roads and the Mena House; the green valley and the harbour for the as-built plateau.

  3. Twelve to twenty stations

    Each rendered today, as built, and as the First Time for the lost-civilisation claim, starting at golden hour.

  4. Inside Khufu

    The descending passage, the Grand Gallery, the King's and Queen's Chambers, the relieving chambers and the subterranean chamber, path traced in granite and limestone from the interior survey already in the database.

  5. Night stations

    The shafts and their stars, Orion over the three, with the live star overlay for 2500 BCE and 10,500 BCE drawn on the night panorama.

  6. Films

    Flights between stations, and the rollback from today to the First Time.
