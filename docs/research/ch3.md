# Chapter 3: Temples of the same stone — research brief

## 1. The claim at full strength

Khafre's Valley Temple and the Sphinx Temple in front of it are built of the Sphinx's own rock. Their limestone core blocks were lifted out of the ditch around the statue, layer by layer, and some weigh a hundred tons or more. Both sides agree on this. So the temples are as old as the ditch, and the ditch, as Schoch argues in chapter 1, shows deep weathering of the kind rain makes.

The temples carry the same weathering. Where the Valley Temple's Aswan granite facing was laid over the limestone core, the core was already rounded and fissured. The masons skinned it back and cut the back of each granite block to sit on the worn surface. Nobody weathers a wall and then cases it. The granite is a refit of a building that was already old.

What ties the temples to Khafre are statues found in a pit and his names around the granite doorways. Both belong to the refit. They show Khafre used the building and leave the age of its core open.

The style points the same way. The building is undecorated, megalithic, and has no parallel at Giza, and its nearest likeness is the Osireion at Abydos. That is another huge, plain building of granite piers and lintels, sunk to the water table, which its excavator Naville first took to be extremely old.

The fair-minded proponent does not deny that Khafre used, cased and enlarged these temples. The claim is narrower: the cores are older than the casing, by enough for rain to have worn them.

## 2. The proponents' evidence

**1. The core blocks are colossal, and they came out of the Sphinx ditch.**
- **Who says it:** Proponents (Schoch, Hancock) use it. Archaeologists assert it themselves:
  - Petrie: the Granite Temple has "a core of megalithic blocks of limestone, ranging in their weight to over a hundred tons each". Petrie, *The Pyramids and Temples of Gizeh* (1883), ch. XIV §95, pp. 128–129 `petrie-1883` **[checked: the online transcription of Petrie's text at ronaldbirdsall.com]**.
  - Lehner: the Sphinx Temple core blocks "weigh up to a hundred tons", and "The blocks had to have come from the U-shaped ditch around the Sphinx". Lehner, "Who Built the Sphinx?", *AERAgram* 18(1), 2017, pp. 2–7 **[checked: the web version on aeraweb.org; the page numbers come from Wikipedia's citation]**.
- **The weights in circulation:**
  - Hancock gives "routinely exceeding 200 tons" in an excerpt credited to *Fingerprints of the Gods* (1995) `hancock-1995` **[unchecked: seen only quoted in a search result; chapter and page not found]**.
  - In 2024 Hancock said "up to 100 tons each": Lex Fridman Podcast #449, transcript at about 01:02:52 **[checked: transcript]**.
  - No published table of block dimensions was found, so no weight is a measurement yet. The 200-ton figure is an outlier against Petrie's and Lehner's "a hundred".
- **How strong:** The observation is solid, because both sides affirm it. As evidence of age it is only as strong as the chapter 1 case for an old ditch. It fails as an age argument if the ditch was quarried under Khafre.

**2. The core blocks show the same precipitation weathering as the ditch walls.**
- **Who says it:** Schoch writes that both temples "show heavy precipitation-induced weathering on their core blocks", weathering that lies "under the Old Kingdom granite veneer". Schoch, *Research Highlights: The Great Sphinx*, robertschoch.com, undated **[checked]**. Not peer reviewed.
- **Where else:** Schoch & McNally, *Voices of the Rocks* (1999) **[unchecked: seen only through a search summary; page not found]**, and Schoch & Bauval, *Origins of the Sphinx* (2017) **[unchecked for this point]**. Neither is peer reviewed.
- **Missing:** No weathering profiles of the temple blocks have been published, peer reviewed or otherwise.
- **How strong:** Weak to moderate. It is wrong if the weathering on show formed after the blocks were exposed. The Valley Temple was cleared by Mariette in the 1850s and 1860s and by Hölscher in 1909–10. The Sphinx Temple was cleared in the 1920s and 1930s. It is also wrong if the weathering comes from salts rather than rain (reply 6).

**3. The granite's back was cut to fit an already-weathered limestone surface.**
- **Who says it:**
  - West, narrating *The Mystery of the Sphinx* (NBC, 1993): "The limestone core-blocks originally had that rolling weathering that you see on the Sphinx … The weathering was cut off and the granite was then married to the limestone core."
  - Schoch in the same film: "there was an old weathered-feature there which was just sort of skinned down", with "the back of the granite was cut to fit the old weathered surface".
  - Both are quoted in Lehner 1994, *KMT* 5(3), 40–48 **[checked: the Hall of Maat reprint]**. The film itself was not seen.
  - Hancock repeats the point in 2024: the core blocks "were already eroded when the granite blocks were put there", and the granite was "deliberately cut to fit into the erosion marks". Lex Fridman #449 **[checked: transcript]**.
- **Missing:** No measured profile of a joint, no photograph series and no drawing of the fit has been published by the proponents.
- **How strong:** This is the chapter's central observation and its least documented. It fails if:
  - the limestone was cut back to receive the granite as the work went along (reply 3), or
  - the joints examined are modern resettings. Lehner says two blocks in Hölscher's façade were replaced after Hölscher photographed it. A Hall of Maat article says much of the facing was reconstructed **[unchecked]**.

**4. Khafre's evidence dates only the refit.**
- **Who says it:**
  - Hancock: the Valley Temple is "attributed to Khafre on no good grounds whatsoever … Yet, it has been remodeled/refaced with granite" (Lex Fridman #449) **[checked]**.
  - Schoch: the temples "were subsequently refurbished with Aswan granite facings during the Old Kingdom" (robertschoch.com) **[checked]**.
- **How strong:** Logically sound as far as it goes. The statues (Mariette 1860) and the doorway inscriptions are on or inside the granite phase. It fails if core and casing were built as one campaign (replies 1 to 3), and if the plan itself (the passage skewed to the causeway, the statue sockets) belongs to Khafre's design.

**5. A dissenting geologist puts the Sphinx Temple before the Valley Temple.**
- **Who says it:** Colin Reader, *Rewriting the History of the Great Sphinx* (Archaeopress, 2026, ISBN 978-1-80583-209-6). Reader analyses the Sphinx Temple's construction phases and disputes that Khafre built it. He holds the Valley Temple to be "of a much later date" and dates the Sphinx's carving to the Second Dynasty **[checked only through reviews: *The Past*, 16 April 2026, and a review summary from *Ancient Egypt* 154; the book was not read]**.
- **Earlier work:** His peer-reviewed paper is Reader, *Archaeometry* 43(1), 2001, 149–165.
- **How strong:** He is the only qualified ally on the temples' sequence, and he gives an older Sphinx Temple by centuries, not millennia. His sequence directly contradicts AERA's (reply 1). The book's review status was not established.

**6. The Osireion is the Valley Temple's twin, and it was taken to be very old.**
- **What is observed:** A sunken, undecorated building at Abydos with granite piers and lintels, standing at the groundwater level.
- **Who says it:**
  - Naville, who excavated it in 1912–14, took it for an Old Kingdom or older structure and compared it with Khafre's "Temple of the Sphinx" **[unchecked: through search summaries and Colavito (2013), who says the "oldest structure" idea "originates, apparently, with Edouard Naville in 1912 and was made famous by Graham Hancock"]**.
  - Naville, "Excavations at Abydos: The Great Pool and the Tomb of Osiris", *JEA* 1 (1914), 2–8 **[unchecked; title and pages differ between listings]**.
  - Hancock 1995 `hancock-1995` **[unchecked: chapter and page not found]**.
  - Westerman (ISIDA) argues it was built in water too deep to have been worked dry. He is not peer reviewed, except as co-author of Parizek et al. 2011 (reply 7).
- **How strong:** Weak. The likeness is real, and archaeologists grant it, but a likeness of style dates nothing. The Osireion's attribution to Seti I rests on excavation (reply 7).

- **The pillars as a test of the tonnages:**
  - `khafre_valley_temple.pillar.across` = 1.01 ± 0.19 m, scaled from Blatt XVII.

  - The residual against Hölscher is 0.03 m, well inside sigma.
  - 1.041² × 4.425 m × 2.7 t/m³ ≈ 12.9 t, so Petrie's 13 tons comes back with a density for granite.
  - This is how to make "100 tons" and "200 tons" receipts rather than adjectives: dimensions × density, with sigma.
  - No density key exists anywhere in `data/`.

## 4. The archaeologists' reply

**1. The building sequence: Valley Temple, then its enclosure wall around the granite, then the Sphinx Temple on that wall; and every block traced to its bed.**
- **The argument:**
  - Khafre's north enclosure wall for the Valley Temple "clearly respects the Temple's granite casing". The Sphinx Temple was built on that wall's foundation, after the casing was finished.
  - The Sphinx Temple's core blocks were cut from "the lowest layers of Member II of the Sphinx quarry". Ricke's 1967–70 study "concluded … that the Sphinx, Sphinx Temple, and Valley Temple were all part of the same quarry and construction process" (AERA, *Khafre's Monuments* **[checked]**).
  - Aigner and Lehner sorted the blocks into types. Most Sphinx Temple blocks are Type A (lower chest beds) and Type C (upper chest and neck beds). Type G, from head level, "form most of the Khafre Valley Temple", so quarrying the enclosure began with the Valley Temple's core (Lehner 2017, AERA web version **[checked]**).
  - The Sphinx Temple was left "incomplete, without its exterior granite casing" (same page), so on its outside there is no granite over weathered limestone at all.
- **Other sources:**
  - AERA, "Sequencing tells us which happened first" (13 October 2009) and "Why sequence is important" **[checked by the chapter 1 brief; the 2009 date is from Wikipedia]**.
  - Lehner, "Reconstructing the Sphinx", *Cambridge Archaeological Journal* 2(1), 1992, 3–26, doi:10.1017/S0959774300000445 **[checked: Crossref record and abstract]**. Peer reviewed.
  - `arce-sphinx-1991`; `lehner-hawass-2017`.
- **How strong:** The strongest reply. It is wrong only if the bed matching is wrong, which has not been challenged in print that I found, or if the whole quarry-and-build sequence is older than Khafre. That is Reader's position, at a few centuries. AERA's pages are not peer reviewed, and the block-by-block typing has never been published as a table.

**2. The temple is built for Khafre's causeway.**
- **Petrie's argument:** The Valley Temple's entrance passage is built with the temple and skewed to meet the causeway. The causeway's direction only makes sense if the Second Pyramid and its upper temple already stood. The order is pyramid, upper temple, causeway, then the Granite Temple, which "was really not built before the reign of Khafra" (Petrie 1883, ch. XIV §99 `petrie-1883` **[checked: transcription]**).
- **Petrie's other points:**
  - The tablet naming a "Temple of the Sphinx" (the Inventory Stela) belongs to a building of the 21st Dynasty, not to this temple.
  - The only datable finds in the temple were statues of Khafra (§99).
- **How strong:** Strong, and 140 years old. A proponent could argue that a new passage was cut into an old block when the causeway was added. Hölscher's and Ricke's drawings of the walls in that zone are where that would be settled.

**3. Core and casing are one technique: the limestone was cut to the granite, not the granite to old weathering.**
- **Lehner's argument:**
  - Hölscher and Ricke document walls built "with a crude core of multi-ton limestone blocks, and finished with a granite casing". The core walls "do not and never did represent finished architecture".
  - In Menkaure's unfinished mortuary temple, "the simultaneous work on core and casing is clear".
  - Where granite has been removed, "the limestone surface cut by the Fourth Dynasty masons to fit the granite casing is still preserved", while the exposed core "weathered in just the last eighty-five years since Hölscher exposed them".
  - He also points to the West–Schoch contradiction: the masons both cut the weathering away and fitted the granite to it.
  - Source: Lehner, "Notes and Photographs on the West–Schoch Sphinx Hypothesis", *KMT* 5(3), Fall 1994, 40–48 **[checked: Hall of Maat reprint]**. Not peer reviewed.
- **Supporting evidence:**
  - Reisner, *Mycerinus* (1931) `reisner-1931` documents Menkaure's limestone core walls and his granite casing, set or only planned. `materials.json` already carries Reisner Plan I's legend with "Black granite casing in situ" and "Emplacements of red granite casing".
  - Menkaure's temple core includes blocks of about 200 tons **[unchecked: from memory, attributed to Lehner, *The Complete Pyramids*; page not found]**. If confirmed, the "unique megalithic style" belongs to a known Fourth Dynasty building tradition.
- **How strong:** Strong in principle, but it rests on photographs and a magazine article. Neither side has published a measured section through the casing joint.

**4. Khafre's name and statues are in the building.**
- **The statues:** Mariette found the seated diorite (gneiss) Khafre, CG 14 / JE 10062, in 1860 in a pit "in well in vestibule" of the Valley Temple, with further statues CG 9–17 and fragments of CG 378 **[checked: search summaries of Wikipedia's *Khafre Enthroned*, the Harvard Digital Giza object record and the Egyptian Museum page; Mariette's own report not identified]**.
- **The inscriptions:** The only surviving inscriptions run round the two entrance doorways, with Khafre's names and titles beside Bastet (north door) and Hathor (south door) **[unchecked: Hawass's page at guardians.net, seen only as a search summary; the page no longer loads. Hölscher's own account in `hoelscher-1912` was not read; page not found]**.
- **The plan:** The hall's statue sockets are drawn on Blatt XVII.
- **How strong:** Decisive on who built the granite phase. On its own, as proponent item 4 says, it does not date the core. Its force comes in combination with replies 1 to 3.

**5. Luminescence dates put the blocks' setting in the third millennium BCE.**
- **The dates:** Sphinx Temple limestone at the base of the outer wall against bedrock, 2220 ± 220 BCE. Valley Temple granite, 3060 ± 470 BCE.
- **Source:** Liritzis & Vafiadou, "Surface luminescence dating of some Egyptian monuments", *Journal of Cultural Heritage* 16 (2015), 134–150, Table 1 p. 137 **[checked by the chapter 1 brief, not by me]**. Peer reviewed.
- **How strong:** Moderate to weak. The method is young, there are few samples and the errors are wide. It dates when a surface was last exposed to light, which for a joint means when the block was set. A limestone core block set c. 2200 ± 220 BCE is hard to square with a block set and worn millennia earlier.

**6. The cores' weathering may be modern and salt-driven.**
- **The argument:** The temples lay under sand until they were cleared (the Valley Temple from the 1850s to 1910, the Sphinx Temple in the 1920s and 1930s). Salt crystallisation fed by dew and groundwater decays both limestone and granite here.
- **Sources:**
  - Gauri, Sinai & Bandyopadhyay, *Geoarchaeology* 10(2), 1995, 119–133 **[checked by the chapter 1 brief: Crossref record]**. Peer reviewed.
  - Harrell, *KMT* 5(2), 1994 **[checked by the chapter 1 brief]**, which notes salt decay of the Valley Temple's granite too.
  - Lehner 1994 on the 85 years of exposure.
- **How strong:** Moderate. Nobody has dated the temple cores' weathering either.

**7. The Osireion is Seti I's, and it floods because the water table rose.**
- **The excavation:** Frankfort's clearance concluded it was built under Seti I. Frankfort, de Buck & Gunn, *The Cenotaph of Seti I at Abydos*, 2 vols, EES Memoir 39 (London, 1933; archive.org gives 1934) **[unchecked: the text was not read; catalogue records only]**.
  - The evidence includes the mark "Menmaatre is beneficial to Osiris" (via Wikipedia).
  - The evidence also includes a Seti I cartouche on a dovetail within the granite construction **[unchecked: via search summary]**.
- **Brand:** the Osireion "can be dated confidently to Seti's reign". Brand, *The Monuments of Seti I* (Leiden: Brill, 2000), pp. 174–178 **[unchecked: via Wikipedia]**.
- **The water:**
  - Abudeif et al. map a water table 5 to 14 m deep, fed by a probable channel from the Nile. *Applied Sciences* 12(20), 10417 (2022), doi:10.3390/app122010417 **[checked: Crossref abstract]**. Peer reviewed. The study is about conservation, not dating.
  - Parizek, Abdel Moneim, Fantle, Westerman & Issawi, "Isotopic data: implications for the source(s) of Osireion groundwater", *EJARS* 1(1), 2011, 61–72, doi:10.21608/ejars.2011.7476 **[checked: Crossref metadata only]**.
- **Also:** Roberson, *JARCE* 43 (2007), 93–112 **[unchecked]**.
- **How strong:** Strong on the date. The resemblance to the Valley Temple is real and is read as Seti's deliberate archaism. Nile-valley groundwater has risen since antiquity, so today's flooding is not evidence of age. Frankfort's own pages should be read before the film states his evidence.

**Newest work found.**
- Lehner's 2016–17 Giza Plateau Mapping Project report (Oriental Institute annual report) says the core blocks "were to be clad in granite or fine limestone". It also says the granite casing and a granite bench were finished before the enclosure wall was built **[unchecked: search snippet; the PDF link redirected]**.
- I found no 2020s peer-reviewed study of either temple's masonry, casing joints or weathering.

## 5. Where it stands

Both sides agree on the stone: the temples' megalithic cores were cut from the Sphinx ditch, and the Valley Temple's granite is Khafre's. The dispute is whether a refit separates the core from the casing. Against a refit stand the sequence AERA reads in the walls, Petrie's causeway geometry, the Menkaure parallel and a handful of third-millennium luminescence dates. For it stand an observation of the casing joint, made on camera in 1993 and never published as a measured section.

The only qualified dissent now in print is Reader's 2026 book. It argues for an older Sphinx Temple, but by centuries, not the millennia of Schoch or Hancock. The decisive test is a measured, photographed section through an original casing joint, and the block-by-block typing published as a table. Neither has been done.
