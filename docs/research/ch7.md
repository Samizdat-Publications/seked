# Chapter 7: Before the flood. Research brief

## 1. The claim at full strength

At the end of the last Ice Age the world went through a sudden catastrophe. Around 12,900 years ago (about 10,950 BCE) the warming stopped and the northern hemisphere fell back into near-glacial cold for twelve centuries: the Younger Dryas. A large, peer-reviewed literature (more than a hundred papers since 2007, by Firestone, Kennett, West, Moore and the Comet Research Group) argues that fragments of a comet set it off. Their evidence is a thin layer of meltglass, microspherules, nanodiamonds and soot found on several continents. One of those sites is the Syrian village of Abu Hureyra, where the evidence is said to show temperatures above 2,200 °C.

When the cold ended, a monument appears almost at once on a hill in south-east Turkey. Göbekli Tepe is a set of great stone rings of T-shaped pillars, up to about 5.5 m tall, carved with animals and with human arms, hands and belts. It dates to about 9,500 BCE, five thousand years before Stonehenge and seven thousand before the pyramids. People who did not yet farm, make pottery or use metal built it. Its own excavators accept that point: the builders were still "hunters and foragers".

Nothing so accomplished arrives without a tradition behind it. One pillar may even record the catastrophe on a date that precession can read. If monumental, symbolic, sky-aware architecture is this old, the idea that a culture could have laid out a plan at Giza in the eleventh millennium BCE stops being absurd. It becomes a question of evidence. That is the proponents' case, and the piece's: Göbekli Tepe is what makes the rest worth taking seriously.

## 2. The proponents' evidence

1. **Builders before farming.** Göbekli Tepe is dated to about 9,500–8,000 cal BC. Its communities left no domesticated plants or animals, so the special buildings' builders "were still living as hunters and foragers".
   - *Who says it:* Schmidt originally. The current DAI director concedes it in Clare (2020), *e-Forschungsberichte des DAI* 2020-2, pp. 81–88, abstract and §2 **[checked]**.
   - *Strength:* very strong. This is the excavators' own position. To be wrong, domesticates would have to turn up in the earliest layer (PPNA). No paper reports them.

2. **Monumental, figurative, standardised architecture.** Round-oval enclosures A–D have T-pillars arranged in rings and two larger central pillars carved as stylised humans. The animal reliefs differ from building to building.
   - *Who says it:* Schmidt (2010), "Göbekli Tepe – the Stone Age Sanctuaries", *Documenta Praehistorica* 37, pp. 239–256 **[unchecked: memory]**. Clare (2020) §2 lists "elaborately carved monolithic T-shaped pillars" **[checked]**.
   - *Strength:* undisputed as a description. The argument lies in what the description implies about the society behind it.

3. **Deliberate burial.** Schmidt held that the enclosures were deliberately and completely filled in during the Stone Age, late in the ninth millennium BC. Hancock reads this as intentional preservation.
   - *Who says it:* Schmidt (2007), "Göbekli Tepe: Stone Age Sanctuaries in Upper Mesopotamia", *Boletín de Arqueología PUCP* 11 **[unchecked: a search summary of the abstract; the publisher page would not load; page not found]**. Hancock (2015), *Magicians of the Gods* (Coronet) **[unchecked: memory; chapter and page not found]**.
   - *Strength:* now weak. Clare (2020) §11 revises it (reply 6). What survives is that the prehistoric population made "mitigation efforts… to protect the special buildings" **[checked, §11]**.

4. **A wider culture, not a one-off.** The Taş Tepeler ("Stone Hills") project has excavated about ten contemporary sites in the Şanlıurfa region since 2019. At Karahan Tepe there are rooms cut into bedrock, pillars and carved heads.
   - *Who says it:* Karul (2021), "Buried Buildings at Pre-Pottery Neolithic Karahantepe", *Türk Arkeoloji ve Etnografya Dergisi* 86 **[unchecked: pages given as 19–31 by vici.org and 21–31 elsewhere; not confirmed]**. Karul's statements that settlement began around 9,400 BCE come from Hürriyet Daily News **[unchecked: press]**.
   - *Strength:* strong for the scale of the phenomenon. Radiocarbon series for Karahan Tepe are not yet in a peer-reviewed paper that I could find.

5. **A cosmic trigger for the Younger Dryas.** A black layer at about 12.9 ka contains magnetic grains with iridium, microspherules, nanodiamonds and fullerenes. It was reported at Clovis-age sites across North America.
   - *Citation:* Firestone et al. (2007), *PNAS* 104(41), pp. 16016–16021, doi:10.1073/pnas.0706977104 **[unchecked: abstract seen via a search summary]**. Peer-reviewed.
   - *Strength:* weak to moderate today. Independent attempts to replicate it largely failed (reply 1).

6. **Abu Hureyra, a Near Eastern village at the boundary.**
   - *The claim:* meltglass formed above 1,720 °C, some perhaps near quartz's boiling point (Fig. 7), with nanodiamonds and spherules. The boundary is modelled at 12,825 ± 55 cal BP.
   - *Citation:* Moore et al. (2020), *Scientific Reports* 10:4185, doi:10.1038/s41598-020-60867-w **[checked, PMC7060197]**. Peer-reviewed.
   - *Status:* corrected on 19 November 2025 (*Sci Rep* 15:40645). The correction covers inconsistencies in Supplementary Table S1 against its original data sources, and adds discussion of the geochronological context **[checked, PMC12630835]**. It has not been retracted. A follow-up, Moore et al. (2023), "Abu Hureyra, Syria, Part 2", appeared in *Airbursts and Cratering Impacts* **[unchecked]**.
   - *Strength:* moderate. It is wrong if the glass is ordinary burnt building earth (reply 3).

7. **Pillar 43, the "Vulture Stone", as a date stamp.** The animal reliefs are read as asterisms, which makes the pillar a record of 10,950 BC ± 250 years, the proposed impact.
   - *Citation:* Sweatman & Tsikritsis (2017), "Decoding Göbekli Tepe with Archaeoastronomy: What Does the Fox Say?", *Mediterranean Archaeology and Archaeometry* 17(1), pp. 233–250 **[unchecked: abstract text seen in a search summary]**. Peer-reviewed, though the journal is not a mainstream archaeology venue.
   - *The 2024 extension:* the V-marks on Pillar 43 are read as a lunisolar calendar of 365 days, with a parallel at Karahan Tepe's 11-pillar pool. Sweatman (2024), *Time and Mind*, doi:10.1080/1751696X.2024.2373876 **[unchecked: the publisher refused the fetch with a 403; the date of 10,850 BC appears only in the Edinburgh press release]**.
   - *Strength:* weak. It rests entirely on which animal is taken to be which constellation (receipt 2).

8. **The YDIH still has defenders in print, up to 2026.**
   - Sweatman, Powell, West (& Young) (2024), "Rebuttal of Holliday et al.'s comprehensive Gish gallop of the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis", *Airbursts and Cratering Impacts* 2(1), doi:10.14293/ACI.2024.0007 **[unchecked]**.
   - Moore et al. (2026), "Systematic Misrepresentations of Younger Dryas Impact Evidence", same journal **[unchecked]**.
   - Hancock leans on this literature: "roughly 100 peer-reviewed studies published since 2006" (Hancock 2023, reply to the SAA, paraphrased) **[checked]**.
   - Note: *Airbursts and Cratering Impacts* is closely associated with the proponents themselves.

9. **Schoch's variant.** Schoch (2012), *Forgotten Civilization* (Inner Traditions), not peer-reviewed, attributes the abrupt end of the Ice Age around 9,700 BCE to solar outbursts, and puts Göbekli Tepe in a following "Solar-Induced Dark Age" **[unchecked: publisher blurb]**. This is the same author as the Sphinx weathering argument in chapter 1 (`dobecki-schoch-1992`).

## 4. The archaeologists' reply

1. **The YDIH is refuted in the main geoscience literature.**
   - Holliday, Daulton, Bartlein, Boslough, Breslawski, Fisher, Jorgeson, Scott, Koeberl, Marlon, Severinghaus, Petaev & Claeys (2023), "Comprehensive refutation of the Younger Dryas Impact Hypothesis (YDIH)", *Earth-Science Reviews* 247:104502, doi:10.1016/j.earscirev.2023.104502. Peer-reviewed **[checked: abstract]**.
   - Their points: "No craters have been identified that date to the onset of the Younger Dryas". The proxies are "nano to microscopic in scale". Very few sites with high levels of any indicator have accurate dating to 12.9 ka.
   - Their 2024 rebuttal of Sweatman et al. stands by this: *ESR* 258:104961 **[unchecked]**.
   - Earlier: Pinter et al. (2011), "The Younger Dryas impact hypothesis: A requiem", *ESR* 106(3), pp. 247–264, and Surovell et al. (2009), *PNAS* 106(43), pp. 18155–18158 **[both unchecked: Wikipedia]**.
   - *Weakness:* the proponents call it a "Gish gallop" and say it misrepresents them. It is a review, not new fieldwork.

2. **Retractions among the Comet Research Group's papers.**
   - Two *PLOS ONE* papers were retracted on 11 February 2026: Baffin Bay (published 6 August 2025), and shocked quartz at Murray Springs, Blackwater Draw and Arlington Canyon (published 10 September 2025). The notices cite missing data, problems with the age model, foraminifera misidentified as comet dust, and undisclosed use of AI. Retraction Watch, 4 March 2026 **[checked]**.
   - The 2021 Tall el-Hammam airburst paper (*Scientific Reports*) was retracted in April 2025 **[checked: Retraction Watch]**.
   - A 2023 Hopewell airburst paper (Tankersley et al., *Sci Rep*) was also retracted **[unchecked: Wikipedia]**.
   - None of these is Firestone 2007, Abu Hureyra 2020 or Sweatman 2017. They bear on the group's methods, not directly on this chapter's papers.

3. **Abu Hureyra's glass can be ordinary.** Thy, Willcox, Barfod & Fuller (2015), *Journal of Archaeological Science*, doi:10.1016/j.jas.2014.11.027, found siliceous scoria droplets only on archaeological sites, with buildings destroyed by fire. They are consistent with burnt building earth at modest temperatures **[unchecked: search summary; volume and pages not confirmed]**. Moore et al. 2020 reply that Thy et al. sampled only interiors **[checked]**.

4. **The crater candidate fell away.** The Hiawatha structure in Greenland, once floated as the Younger Dryas impactor, was dated to about 58 million years ago. Kenny et al. (2022), *Science Advances* 8(10):eabm2434 **[unchecked: Wikipedia]**.

5. **Pillar 43 does not fit the dates.**
   - Notroff, Dietrich, Clare, Dietrich, Schlindwein, Kinzel, Lelek-Tvetmarken & Sönmez (2017), "More than a vulture: A response to Sweatman and Tsikritsis", *MAA* 17(2), pp. 57–63, doi:10.5281/zenodo.581724 **[checked: the authors' DAI blog version]**. The journal printed it as a peer-reviewed response.
   - Their points:
     - 10,950 BC is 700–1,000 years older than the oldest radiocarbon date for Enclosure D: "any available date for Göbekli Tepe rather marks the end than the beginning of the Younger Dryas."
     - Many pillars are not in their original positions, and the buildings may have been roofed.
     - There is no reason to assume Greek or Arabian constellations.
     - The authors chose a few pillars out of more than 60.
     - The "headless man" carries an emphasised phallus.
   - Sweatman & Tsikritsis replied in the same issue, pp. 63–70 **[unchecked]**.

6. **The site has been reinterpreted from the inside: houses, water, and slope slides.** Clare (2020), DAI report, not peer-reviewed but the excavators' official summary, says deep soundings have "exposed the weaknesses of the temple-narrative" (§4) **[checked]**:
   - **Houses:** PPNA round-oval houses on bedrock, with hearths and bead-making (§5). Rectangular EPPNB buildings, possibly dense domestic occupation (§7). A subfloor burial of at least three people (§8).
   - **Water:** a rock-cut pit 8.0 m across and 2.8 m deep, a possible cistern, with a slab-covered channel cut into bedrock 12 m away (§9).
   - **Dates:** mortar dates show the special buildings were in use more than five centuries. Round and rectangular phases overlap, so Schmidt's three-layer sequence is abandoned (§10).
   - **The fill:** earlier work "postulated an intentional (ritually-motivated) backfilling", but the evidence "now suggests an inundation by displaced archaeological deposits from adjacent higher-lying knolls", as multiple slope-slide events (§11, Fig. 7). This explains the mixed PPNA and PPNB fill and the scattered radiocarbon dates.

7. **Cereal processing on site.** More than 7,000 ground-stone artefacts, standardised and mostly used on grain. Phytoliths confirm cereals. Dietrich et al. (2019), *PLOS ONE* 14(5):e0215214. Peer-reviewed **[unchecked: abstract summarised by search, not fetched]**. These are wild cereals, harvested intensively. That does not contradict "before farming", but it removes the picture of "nomads visiting a temple".

8. **"Monumental — compared to what?"** Kinzel & Clare (2020), in Gebauer et al. (eds.), *Monumentalising Life in the Neolithic*, Oxbow, pp. 29–48 **[unchecked: pages from JSTOR's contents via search]**. The excavators question the scale framing itself.

9. **It does not bear on Giza.**
   - The SAA's open letter to Netflix and ITN, 30 November 2022, signed by its president Daniel Sandweiss, asked for the series to be reclassified and given disclaimers. Its phrase: "we find no archaeological evidence to support the existence of an 'advanced, global Ice Age civilization'" **[unchecked: the PDF at documents.saa.org failed on TLS; date and addressees confirmed by Hancock's reply title; quote via the Daily Caller and the SAA Archaeological Record, May 2023]**.
   - Göbekli is a hunter-gatherer achievement with local successors. It offers no material link to Egypt, which shows no comparable monumental construction until the fourth millennium BCE.
   - **Hancock's answer** (Hancock 2023, grahamhancock.com, 30 January 2023) **[checked]**: "absence of evidence is not evidence of absence". Large areas are unsurveyed. Archaeologists are not suppressing evidence, but their preconceptions shape what they find credible. The racism charge is "a spurious attempt to smear by association" (Colavito's quotation of the reply). The series' catastrophe rests on the YDIH literature.

## 5. Where it stands

- **Göbekli Tepe's age is settled.** It is monumental, carved architecture built around 9,500 BCE by people without domesticated crops or animals, and the excavators agree. Their post-2019 work makes the builders more settled, with houses, cisterns and grain, and makes the burial look more like landslip than ritual. It does not make the monuments any younger.
- **The Younger Dryas impact is a minority position.** It is under heavy, peer-reviewed criticism, and several of the group's papers have been retracted in 2025–26, though its core papers stand and its authors keep publishing.
- **Pillar 43 and Giza are the weakest links.** Göbekli's dates mark the end of the Younger Dryas, not its beginning, and nothing material connects it to Giza 1,100 km away. Göbekli shows that monumental building is older than the textbooks once allowed. It does not show that Giza is.
