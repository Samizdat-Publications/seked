## 1. The claim at full strength

Egypt did not remember itself as starting with Menes. Every native chronology that survives puts long ages before the first king. The Ramesside Turin Canon lists gods, then the *akhu* (spirits) and the Followers of Horus, and gives them more than 13,420 and more than 23,200 years. Manetho, as Eusebius and Syncellus transmit him, gives thousands of years of gods, demigods and spirits of the dead. The Egyptians called the founding age *sp tpj*, the First Time. They placed it in the Memphite region, and their kings re-enacted it.

The Pyramid Texts of Unas, carved c. 2400 BCE, are already old in their language and ideas. They send the dead king to the sky as a star, with Orion (Sah, who is Osiris) and Sothis. The Followers of Horus may be a real memory of a predynastic elite of astronomer-priests. The Edfu Building Texts describe divine builders who came from a homeland destroyed by a flood and laid out the first temples.

A Saite stela says the Sphinx and a temple of Isis already stood when Khufu came. The Egyptian sky's own clock, precession, puts the belt of Orion at its lowest, the start of its cycle, around 10,500 BCE. That is the age a "First Time of Osiris-Orion" would describe. None of these is proof alone. Together they make a coherent case that dynastic Egypt inherited a tradition, and perhaps a site plan, much older than itself, and remembered that it had.

## 2. The proponents' evidence

1. **The Turin Canon puts thousands of years of spirits before Menes.** Gardiner's col. II (row 3.8 in the pharaoh.se numbering) reads "…spirits (*Ꜣḫw*), Followers of Horus (*šmsw-Ḥr*), 13,420 + x years". The next row reads "…up to the Followers of Horus, 23,200 + x years". Menes heads the following line. Helck's restorations are cited for the nearby rows.
   - **Who uses it:** H&B 1996 pp. 213–214, citing Schwaller de Lubicz, *Sacred Science* p. 86. H&B argue (p. 214) that *akhu* means "Shining Ones", not "Venerables". The total of 36,620 years is Schwaller's sum; it is not written on the papyrus.
   - **Checked:** the transliteration at pharaoh.se, Turin col. 3 **[checked]**; H&B **[checked against the scan]**; Schwaller **[unchecked, cited by H&B]**.
   - **Strength:** the numerals are partly read. The pharaoh.se reading calls the two ten-thousand signs "very probable", and a Turin museum catalogue it quotes says Champollion read 14,420, and 23,200 or 24,204.
   - **What would make it wrong:** the papyrus was written c. 1250 BCE, about 1,850 years after Menes. Its pre-Menes section would have to be a theological schema, not a record.

2. **Manetho gives the same picture.** In Eusebius's Armenian version (Fr. 1, Waddell 1940 pp. 3–9), the gods reign 13,900 years "down to Bydis". Demigods follow for 1,255 years, then kings of Memphis and This for 1,817 years, then spirits of the dead and demigods for 5,813. The whole rule is "24,900 lunar years, which make 2206 solar years" (p. 7). Syncellus gives the gods 11,985 years (Fr. 2–3, pp. 11–17), and the "Old Chronicle" he transmits gives 36,525 years (p. 227).
   - **Who uses it:** H&B p. 216.
   - **Checked:** Waddell **[checked, LacusCurtius transcription of the Loeb]**. The 36,525 at p. 227 **[unchecked, from H&B's note 21]**.
   - **Strength:** Eusebius himself turned the years into 30-day months. The totals disagree between transmitters. The source is Ptolemaic, about 280 BCE, and survives only in Christian chronographers of the 4th to 9th centuries CE.

3. **Greek visitors heard the same long chronology.** Herodotus 2.142 reports 341 generations, 11,340 years. Diodorus 1.44 reports a little under 18,000 years of gods and heroes and under 5,000 of mortal kings.
   - **Who uses it:** H&B p. 217.
   - **Checked:** **[unchecked]**. Diodorus is cited by H&B to the Loeb vol. I p. 157; the Herodotus figures are from memory.
   - **Strength:** this is independent hearsay from Late Period priests, so it shows what they believed, not what happened.

4. **The Inventory Stela.** This is Cairo JE 2091, found by Mariette in 1858 in the Isis temple at the queen's pyramid G1-c. In Breasted's translation it says Khufu "found the house of Isis, Mistress of the Pyramid, beside the house of the Sphinx". It also says he rebuilt her temple.
   - **Who uses it:** H&B p. 24 n. 24: if the stela is genuine, Khafre cannot have built the Sphinx. Schoch & Bauval 2017 argue it copies a genuine Old Kingdom text.
   - **Checked:** Breasted 1906 §§177–180, pp. 83–85 **[checked against Colavito's transcription]**. Schoch & Bauval **[checked via Colavito's review, not the book]**.
   - **Strength:** weak. The proponent has to show an Old Kingdom original behind a text that is late in its spelling and in its theology.

5. **The Edfu Building Texts.** These are Ptolemaic, c. 200 BCE by H&B's date. As Reymond reads them, they describe the "Homeland of the Primeval Ones", an island destroyed by a flood. Survivors, the Seven Sages and the Builder Gods, founded the first temple on the primeval mound. Reymond took them to preserve a predynastic tradition from near Memphis, probably Heliopolis.
   - **Who uses it:** H&B ch. 12, pp. 202–212. They cite Reymond pp. 27–31, 39, 44, 46, 48, 55, 90, 109, 112–113, 127 and 257, and compare the Apkallu and the Rishis. Hancock 2015 (*Magicians of the Gods*) ties the flood to the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis, c. 10,800 BCE (page not found). Schoch & Bauval 2017 treat the texts as history.
   - **Checked:** H&B **[checked]**. Reymond's pages are as H&B cite them **[unchecked]**.
   - **Strength:** it rests on Reymond's reading. A Ptolemaic cosmogony would have to be shown to carry dated historical content.

6. **Zep Tepi as a remembered golden age.** H&B (ch. 8, pp. 146–148) call Zep Tepi "a mysterious and wonderful golden age that had immediately followed Creation". They place it in the Memphite region and cite Rundle Clark 1959 pp. 263–265, PT Utt. 600 and Frankfort's *Kingship and the Gods*. In ch. 14 (p. 220ff) they date it by precession to "the Age of Leo, circa 10,500 BC".
   - **Checked:** H&B **[checked]**; Rundle Clark **[unchecked]**.
   - **Strength:** the dating step is the proponents' own. No Egyptian text attaches a date or a sky to *sp tpj*.

7. **Unas's Pyramid Texts make the king a star with Orion.** These are Unas's own walls, the oldest copy, carved c. 2450–2363 BCE (95% range, Bronk Ramsey 2010). Sethe's numbering, location in the tomb, and the line:
   - **PT 214 §139**, sarcophagus chamber, south wall: "the Imperishable Stars have carried you".
   - **PT 215 §141**, same wall: "There is no god who has become a star without a companion."
   - **PT 216 §151**, same wall: "Orion is encircled by the Duat… Sothis is encircled by the Duat… This Unas is encircled by the Duat."
   - **PT 219 §186**, Osiris litany: "in your name of He-in-Orion".
   - **PT 248 §262–263**, antechamber, west gable: "Unas appears as a star."
   - **PT 263 §341**, antechamber: "His sister is Sothis."
   - **PT 268 §374**, antechamber: "leads the Imperishable Stars".
   - **PT 273–274 §405, §408**, the Cannibal Hymn: "the Great Ones in the Northern Sky", and "Orion, the father of the gods".
   - **PT 302 §458–459**, antechamber, north wall: "Sothis lives… as the Great Bear which cannot perish".
   - **Checked:** all of the above against pyramidtextsonline.com, a modernised Piankoff translation **[checked]**.
   - **The best-known Orion lines are not in Unas.** PT 442 §819c, "Behold, he is come as Sah; behold, Osiris is come as Sah" (Mercer 1952 **[checked]**; Faulkner 1969 renders Sah as "Orion" **[unchecked]**), is attested first for Pepi I (TLA **[checked]**). PT 466 §882, "you are this great star, the companion of Orion", is the line Bauval quotes most **[unchecked; the attestation is probably Pepi I or later, not Unas]**.
   - **Who uses it:** Bauval 1989; `bauval-gilbert-1994`; H&B pp. 140–145 and 169–171, which cite §§151, 820, 882 and others.
   - **Strength:** strong as evidence of an Old Kingdom star religion. It is evidence of a 10,500 BCE date only if one grants the precessional step in item 6.

8. **The Followers of Horus as a real elite.** H&B ch. 11 (pp. 195–201), citing Emery 1961 pp. 22–23, argue for a historical astronomer-priest elite at Heliopolis. **[checked: H&B]**
   - **Strength:** Emery's "dynastic race" hypothesis, which this leans on, has since been abandoned by archaeology.

The newest proponent work in this chapter's scope recycles the 1990s arguments: Schoch & Bauval 2017; Hancock 2015 (updated 2017); and *Ancient Apocalypse* on Netflix (2022, 2024), which does not centre on Egypt. ## 4. The archaeologists' reply

1. **Absolute chronology is now independent of the king lists.**
   - **The evidence:** Bronk Ramsey et al. (2010, *Science* 328:1554–1557, peer-reviewed) used 211 short-lived plant samples in a Bayesian model. Dee et al. (2013, *Proc. R. Soc. A* 469:20130395, peer-reviewed) put Aha's accession at 3111–3045 BCE (68%) and the whole Naqada sequence at about 600–700 years. Hornung, Krauss & Warburton (2006, Brill HdO 83; chronological table p. 490) is the standard historical framework.
   - **What it shows:** a dense, continuous material record from Naqada to the Old Kingdom, with no gap where a civilisation of 10,500 BCE would sit.
   - **Checked:** **[checked]** for all three, the Hornung table page from the publisher's contents only.
   - **What would make it wrong:** systematic contamination across hundreds of samples. The paper itself reports that an earlier large study (its ref. 26) gave Old Kingdom dates "substantially earlier" than expected. But that offset is in centuries, not millennia.

2. **The Inventory Stela is late and anachronistic.**
   - **Breasted 1906 §177:** "the orthographic evidences of its late date are entirely conclusive". He also rejects it as a copy of an older document **[checked via transcription]**.
   - **Other anachronisms:** Isis is not securely attested before Nyuserre of the 5th Dynasty. "Mistress of the Pyramids" is the title of the Third Intermediate and Late Period Isis temple that grew out of G1-c's chapel. The Sphinx's names Hurun and Horemakhet are New Kingdom. (Jánosi 1996 pp. 11, 125; Zivie-Coche 1991, 2002; Fagan 2006 pp. 111–112 **[unchecked, via Wikipedia]**.)
   - **The rest of the reading:** it was a priestly foundation legend for a temple competing for prestige.
   - **What would make it wrong:** an Old Kingdom original turning up.

3. **Zep Tepi is mythic time, not a date.**
   - **The evidence:** Graham (2022, *Birmingham Egyptology Journal* 9:52–84, abstract p. 52 **[checked]**; a student-run journal, so lightly peer-reviewed) describes the *sp tpj* as "a pristine creative epoch" which, "in its atemporal extension as ḏ.t", is a template "brought closer in physical reality by performing *mꜢꜤ.t*" in ritual. Assmann and Hornung say the same (**[unchecked]**).
   - **What it means:** every coronation and temple foundation re-enacts the First Time. Asking when it happened misreads the category.
   - **Strength:** strong. H&B's own source, Rundle Clark, presents it as myth.

4. **The Followers of Horus and the king-list numbers are ritual and schematic categories.**
   - **In the Old Kingdom:** *šms Ḥr* is a biennial royal progress, a tax and cattle count, recorded on the Palermo Stone (Wilkinson 2000 **[unchecked]**; Nuzzolo et al. 2021).
   - **In later tradition:** the Followers are the collective royal ancestors, as with the Souls of Pe and Nekhen.
   - **The numbers themselves:** the Turin figures are partly restored, follow a mythic sequence of gods, and were written about 1,850 years after Menes. Manetho's totals differ between transmitters, and Eusebius already reduced them to months. The Old Chronicle's 36,525 is 25 × 1,461, a whole number of Sothic cycles, which signals schematic arithmetic. (The factorisation is my arithmetic; the "Sothic" reading is **[unchecked]**.)
   - **Sources:** Gardiner 1959; Helck 1992; Ryholt 2004 (*Ägypten und Levante* 14:135–155, peer-reviewed) is the modern reconstruction **[bibliography checked; content unchecked]**.

5. **The Edfu texts are Ptolemaic theology.**
   - **The reading:** the island rising from the waters is the primeval mound coming out of Nun, the standard Egyptian creation image. The temple's founding myth is a late theological composition about the temple as the cosmos (Finnestad 1985; the Edfu Project translations under Kurth **[both unchecked]**).
   - **Reymond herself:** she argued for a Memphite or Heliopolitan predynastic source, but she gave no date and no lost land overseas.
   - **Strength:** moderate. Reymond's "older source" thesis is respectable; the leap to a 10,500 BCE flood is not hers.

6. **The star religion is real, and it describes the sky of its own time.**
   - **The scholarship:** Faulkner 1966 (*JNES* 25:153–161, peer-reviewed **[existence checked; content unchecked]**) established the stellar layer of the Pyramid Texts: the king joins the circumpolar "Imperishable Stars" in the north and travels with Sah and Sothis. Krauss 1997 (ÄA 59, peer-reviewed monograph **[bibliography checked; content unchecked]**) reconstructs these as observations of the Old Kingdom sky: Orion and Sirius rising before dawn in the eastern Duat and Field of Reeds. Allen 2005 / 2015 (SBL WAW) reads the Duat as the twilight realm of rebirth; the Piankoff-based text above glosses Duat as "Twilight" (§341).
   - **What it implies:** nothing in the texts points to 10,500 BCE. C2's shafts show the same sky in about 2490 BCE.
   - **Critiques already in the database:** `krupp-1997` (the inversion of north and south) and `fairall-1999` (the belt's angle) hit the precessional step directly.

7. **The wider profession's reply.** The Society for American Archaeology's open letter to Netflix of 30 November 2022 objected to *Ancient Apocalypse* being presented as factual **[seen via search only]**. Colavito's 2017 review of Schoch & Bauval (not peer-reviewed) argues that they treat myth as history and that their stela argument contradicts their own Khufu-face argument **[checked]**.

## 5. Where it stands

The proponents are right on the facts that matter for the story. Egypt's own records put long ages of gods and spirits before Menes. The oldest religious corpus makes the king a star with Orion and Sothis. Ptolemaic and Saite priests told stories of an older Giza.

Every one of those texts was written long after what it describes, and none carries a date. The archaeological and radiocarbon record, which is independent of the texts and peer-reviewed, runs continuously from Naqada to Khufu with no room for an earlier builder civilisation. The only route from the First Time to 10,500 BCE is the precessional step, and Seked's own C4 and C5 compute it as failing.

## 6. What the picture should show

1. **The papyrus becomes a timeline.** The Turin Canon fragment (col. II), with "13,420 + x" and "23,200 + x" lit. A timeline unrolls backward from Menes at 3085 BCE, past 10,500 BCE to about 39,700 BCE, labelled with where each list's total lands (Turin, Manetho, Herodotus).
2. **Unas's walls become the sky.** The blue hieroglyphs and star ceiling of Unas's burial chamber at Saqqara, with PT 216 §151 ("Orion is encircled by the Duat… Sothis… this Unas") highlighted. Then the night sky over Saqqara in about 2400 BCE, Sah and Sothis rising in the east, and the king's soul joining them.
3. **The stela beside the Sphinx.** The Inventory Stela, with the Khufu, "house of Isis" and "house of the Sphinx" line picked out, set beside the Sphinx in its ditch.

**For the reply:** the same timeline, but with each text pinned to when it was written: Pyramid Texts about 2400 BCE, Turin about 1250, Inventory Stela about 670, Manetho about 280, Edfu about 200 BCE. Each draws an arrow back to what it claims. Behind them, the radiocarbon band (Dee 2013, Bronk Ramsey 2010) fills 3200–2300 BCE and stays empty at 10,500.
