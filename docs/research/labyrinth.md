## 1. The claim at full strength

Herodotus saw it and said it surpassed the pyramids:

- twelve roofed courts and 3,000 rooms;
- half of the rooms underground, kept from him as the tombs of kings and sacred crocodiles.

Strabo saw it too. Since Petrie it has been placed south of Amenemhat III's pyramid at Hawara. Petrie found only a bed of limestone chips, about 305 × 244 m, and declared the building quarried away. But over most of the site he turned over only 0.6–1.2 m of chips and sand.

In 2008 Egypt's own national geophysics institute surveyed the ground south of the pyramid, with the antiquities authority's inspectors on site. The team reported thick walls and nearly closed rooms 8–12 m down, across hectares. A peer-reviewed paper by that institute's team (2010) found a grid of square and elongated anomalies that "significantly matches" Herodotus. The organiser says the authority then barred further publicity.

Satellite radar later found returns that the visible surface does not explain, on both sides of the canal and over a larger area than Petrie allowed. Rising saline groundwater is destroying whatever is there. In May 2026, digging reportedly began.

A fair proponent does not claim that this dates Giza. The claim is narrower: one of antiquity's most famous buildings, whose underground half no modern person has entered, may survive beneath a field 90 km from Giza, and nobody has looked properly. If the underground half of a wonder can go unexamined for 2,400 years, "it would have been found by now" is not an argument.

## 2. The proponents' evidence

1. **Two eyewitnesses describe a vast building with an underground half.**
   - *Herodotus*, *Histories* II.148: twelve roofed courts, six facing north and six facing south; 3,000 chambers, 1,500 above ground and 1,500 below; the lower ones shown to him by no one, being "the burial vaults of the kings who first built this labyrinth, and of the sacred crocodiles"; a pyramid of "two hundred and forty feet" at one corner.
   - *Strabo*, *Geography* 17.1.37: as many courts as there were nomes; roofs of single stones; colonnades of 27 monolithic pillars; the tomb pyramid "each side about 4 plethra".
   - *Status:* **[checked]** as quoted in Carlotto 2025, pp. 408–410. The translation is not named there, and I did not read the Greek or a Loeb edition.
   - *Pliny:* *Natural History* 36.84–89 reportedly says it was built "three thousand six hundred years ago, they say" (**[unchecked]**: Wikipedia, "Labyrinth of Egypt").
   - *Strength:* strong that a great building stood here. Weak on the underground half, which Herodotus reports as hearsay. It is wrong if his "underground chambers" were the pyramid's substructure and the crocodile burials, which is what he was told they were.

2. **Petrie's "nothing left" rests on shallow digging.**
   - *What Petrie did:* in 1911 he cleared chips and earth 15–24 ft (4.6–7.3 m) deep along the pyramid's south foot. Elsewhere he turned over only "2 to 4 feet" (0.6–1.2 m) of chips and sand (Petrie 1912, §41 **[checked]**).
   - *What he found in 1888:* five blocks in situ on a bed of limestone chips; chips 10–12 ft thick in one place and about 6 ft in another; a double pavement, 25 in of yellow limestone under 15 in of fine white (Petrie 1889, §§6–7 **[checked]**).
   - *The proponents' reading:* De Cordier argues that Petrie's surface was a roof or upper level, not a foundation (Substack, 2025 **[checked]**).
   - *Strength:* moderate as a point about what has not been dug. It is wrong if the bedrock and the water table leave no room for a deep storey; see reply 4.

3. **The 2008 NRIAG survey: the Mataha Expedition.**
   - *Who ran it:* privately funded and organised by Louis De Cordier, a Belgian artist. The geophysics was run by NRIAG under Dr Abbas Mohamed Abbas, with SCA inspectors present, from mid-February to March 2008.
   - *Methods:* GPR, ERT, GEM and VLF.
   - *What was reported:* walls and nearly closed rooms "8 to 12 meters below the surface", "some several meters thick", spanning "hectares" south of the pyramid, with saline groundwater at 4–5 m. Abbas is quoted on "a massive wall beside the pyramid, extending straight down over 17 meters deep".
   - *Sources:* De Cordier, "Unveiling the Lost Labyrinth" (24 March 2025) and "The Mataha-Expedition Part I" (21 July 2025), Substack **[checked]** as the author's account.
   - *Ghent University:* named as a partner, but in his posts De Cordier names no Ghent scientist. One version of his report is titled "Mataha Expedition Hawara 2008 NRIAG – Ghent University/Kunst-Zicht" (**[unchecked]**: search-result title; Kunst-Zicht is a Ghent art space). The results were reportedly presented at the Aula of Ghent University (photo caption, "The Mataha Team", **[checked]**), in October 2008 according to one secondary site (**[unchecked]**).
   - *Publication:* De Cordier dates the NRIAG report to April 2008 and links it as a PDF. I could not read it. I found no NRIAG journal article from 2008. Even Carlotto, who is sympathetic, cites the 2008 work only as "Courdier, 2008", meaning the Substack post (Carlotto p. 420 **[checked]**).
   - *Strength:* weak as published evidence: it is unreviewed and reported second-hand by the organiser. The depths and the 17 m wall have not been published by the geophysicists themselves (see reply 3). The measured part was peer reviewed in 2010 (item 4).

4. **The peer-reviewed version: Khalil et al. (2010).**
   - *The paper:* Khalil, Abbas, Monteiro Santos, Mesbah and Massoud, *Near Surface Geophysics* 8(3), 203–212. This is the NRIAG team from 2008.
   - *Method:* VLF-EM on a 2 m grid, with nine Schlumberger soundings inverted in 3D.
   - *Results:* "a large group of elongated and square subsurface anomalies at shallow depths", whose distribution "significantly matches" Herodotus's description (abstract **[checked]**).
   - *Strength:* the strongest item on this side, because it is measured and refereed. Its weaknesses are its own words, "shallow depths", and a survey grid only about 140 × 100 m (Carlotto Fig. 3 **[checked]**, as registered by Carlotto). It is wrong if the anomalies are the Roman town Petrie identified (reply 2) or the foundations Petrie already found.

5. **"Publication was stopped."**
   - *What De Cordier says:* after the Ghent presentation, the SCA under Zahi Hawass "ordered an information blackout and blocked further research" (Substack "About" page **[checked]**). Another of his posts says only that the findings "faced restrictions on public disclosure due to national security concerns", without saying who imposed them or when (Part I, footnote 8 **[checked]**). The Archaeological Rescue Foundation (ARF) says Hawass threatened "national security sanctions" (archaeologicalrescue.org/hawara **[checked]**).
   - *Carlotto:* repeats that the SCA "prohibited any group from releasing further information collected at the site", citing De Cordier (pp. 412–413 **[checked]**).
   - *Strength:* weak. I found no SCA document, no press report from 2008–10 and no statement from NRIAG. The scientists published in 2010, 2023 and 2024 (reply 3).

6. **The Merlin Burrows satellite scans (2015, released 2025): the "under the pyramid" claim.**
   - *Who and where:* Tim Akers of Merlin Burrows, a UK firm, now deceased, presented them in Harrogate on 21 July 2015. They were held under a 10-year non-disclosure agreement and published by De Cordier on 6 June 2025 ("The Merlin Burrows Scans II" **[checked]**).
   - *What is claimed:*
     - a central atrium about 40 × at least 100 m, an erratum lowering the earlier 64 × 160 m;
     - four underground layers 20–50 m apart;
     - a complex "the size of ten football fields";
     - an object about 40 m long ("Dippy");
     - shafts about 30 m deep with side chambers up to 8 × 10 m;
     - an Ω-shaped moat;
     - "2 chambers below the Hawara Pyramid", one matching Petrie's burial chamber and one "beyond his blind passage".
   - *Depths:* ARF gives patterns at 40, 60, 80 and 100 m (**[checked]**). The method is not disclosed; De Cordier calls it a fusion of many satellite passes, coloured by depth.
   - *Strength:* very weak. There is no method, no data and no review, and depths of 40–100 m are far beyond the 0.5–5 m that even the sympathetic Carlotto gives for satellite radar (reply 4).

7. **Satellite radar shows anomalies south of the pyramid (Carlotto 2025).**
   - *The paper:* Mark J. Carlotto (General Dynamics IT, writing independently), *Journal of Scientific Exploration* 39(4), 407–422, December 2025, CC BY-NC 4.0.
   - *Data:* Sentinel-1 C-band (6 July 2023) and ALOS PALSAR L-band (17 August 2007), compared with a Google Earth image of 6 February 2007.
   - *Results:* principal components analysis and a "nonlinear background estimation" find "two distinct rectangular areas south of the pyramid on either side of the Abdul Wahbi canal" that the optical image does not predict (pp. 416–417, Figs. 8, 9, 11, 15). The eastern area is "approximately 450,000 square feet" (≈41,800 m²), "more than twice Petrie's estimate", and one of the two NRIAG grids falls inside it (p. 419).
   - *His own caveats:* the returns "may be from the legendary Labyrinth, or from material uncovered when the canal was built" (p. 420).
   - *Status:* all **[checked]**. JSE is the journal of the Society for Scientific Exploration, and its masthead reads "Anomalistics and Frontier Science" (p. 407 **[checked]**). That it is peer reviewed is **[unchecked]**.
   - *Strength:* weak to moderate. It is wrong if the decorrelation comes from surface roughness, soil moisture, or the spoil and town debris along the canal. Carlotto's own penetration figures (C-band 0.5 m, L-band 2–5 m, p. 415) put anything he sees within a few metres of the surface, so it is not the 8–12 m walls or the 40–100 m levels.

8. **Fieldwork began in 2026.**
   - Focus.it (Luigi Bignami, 14 May 2026 **[checked]**) reports that the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities confirmed new excavation at Hawara on 7 May 2026. It credits pressure from De Cordier's Mataha Foundation and from ARF, whose groundwater plan is dated February 2026.
   - ARF reports a "megalithic stone wall" uncovered in the central area (**[checked]** as ARF's claim). Ancient History X names Ashraf Sobhi, Director General of Fayoum Antiquities, as the supervisor (**[checked]** as that site's claim).
   - I found no ministry press release or Ahram Online report.
   - *Strength:* if confirmed, this is the test itself. So far nothing has been published.

**A note for the script.** As far as I found, Hancock, Bauval, Schoch and West have not published a case on Hawara. This claim's proponents are De Cordier, Merlin Burrows (Akers), ARF (Trevor Grassi, William Brown), Carlotto and Carmen Boulter's *The Pyramid Code* (**[unchecked]**: named by De Cordier and ARF). ## 4. The archaeologists' reply (strongest first)

1. **The Labyrinth is a known Twelfth Dynasty temple, and says nothing about Giza's age.**
   - *The finds:* Petrie found architraves inscribed for Amenemhat III and Sobekneferu, granite shrines with statues of Amenemhat III, and a south gate on the pyramid's axis (Petrie 1889 §7; Petrie 1912 §§41–43 **[checked]**).
   - *Its history:* Petrie dates its ruin to soon after 193 BCE, when it was still in royal care under Cleopatra I (1912, §42 **[checked]**). Amenemhat III reigned around 1855–1808 BCE (Elshazly 2024, p. 40 **[checked]**).
   - *Later work on the identification:*
     - Lloyd, "The Egyptian Labyrinth", *JEA* 56 (1970), 81–100 (**[unchecked]**: cited in Carlotto's references);
     - Arnold, "Das Labyrinth und seine Vorbilder", *MDAIK* 35 (1979), 1–9, which reinterprets Lepsius and Petrie by comparison with Amenemhat III's Dahshur complex (**[unchecked]**: a summary of a 2002 *Bibliotheca Orientalis* review, which returned 403);
     - Uphill, *Pharaoh's Gateway to Eternity* (2000) (**[unchecked]**);
     - Blom-Böer's Leiden doctoral thesis on the temple (2004, **[checked]** catalogue only; the full text is under embargo).
   - *Ancient attribution:* Manetho reportedly credits the Labyrinth to the Twelfth Dynasty king Lamares (**[unchecked]**, memory).
   - Even a fully preserved lower storey would be a Middle Kingdom building, some 700 years after Khufu.

2. **What Lepsius took for the Labyrinth was a Roman town, and the modern canal cut through the site.**
   - Petrie showed that Lepsius's brick chambers were "only the ruins of the Roman town of the destroyers" (1912, §41 **[checked]**).
   - Kraemer (2010, p. 371) describes the medieval canal, about 30 m wide, 13 m deep and 1 km long, cut through mud-brick, Labyrinth debris and bedrock, with a settlement of the 3rd century BCE to the 1st century CE built on the temple's foundations (**[checked]** as quoted by Carlotto pp. 418–419).
   - Near-surface radar and EM anomalies over this ground are what a town over chips would give. Carlotto concedes the settlement reading (p. 420 **[checked]**).

3. **The 2008 team kept publishing, cautiously, which tells against both a suppression and a find.**
   - *2010:* Khalil, Abbas et al. call the anomalies shallow (abstract **[checked]**).
   - *2023:* Abbas et al. treat Hawara as a groundwater-hazard case study (Springer Geology chapter, pp. 287–312, **[unchecked]**: from Elshazly's references).
   - *2024:* Elshazly, Massoud, Abdeldayem, El-Khodary and **Abbas M. Abbas**, the leader of the 2008 survey, ran 25 ERT profiles, including across the Labyrinth area. They report high-resistivity "closures" 6 m wide at about 7.5 m depth and 4 m wide at 5 m depth (p. 44, Figs. 4 and 6). These "may indicate the presence of archaeological remains, which require further verification through standard excavation methods" (p. 51 **[checked]**).
   - Nothing in this record matches 8–12 m walls across hectares, a 17 m wall, or a second storey. No source shows publication being stopped.

4. **Bedrock, groundwater and physics limit what can be down there and what can be seen.**
   - *Bedrock and sediment:* the tomb lies in a trench cut into Eocene mudstone, with limestone at 12–15 m and 3–5 m of Holocene gravel and sand above (Szynkiewicz 2015, p. 385 **[checked]**).
   - *Groundwater:* 1.5–10 m below the surface (Elshazly 2024 **[checked]**); about 5 m according to the Leeds/NRIAG project (**[checked]**, project page); 2–7 m by TEM (Massoud et al. 2010, *Chinese Journal of Geophysics* 53(3), 638–645, **[unchecked]**: search snippet).
   - *Flooding:* the tomb chambers are flooded today (Szynkiewicz p. 385). Petrie found the burial chamber's water "not more than chest deep" in 1889 (1890, §6 **[checked]**).
   - *Radar penetration:* conductive, saline, wet clay kills GPR. Satellite radar reaches 0.5 m at C-band and 2–5 m at L-band even in dry sand (Carlotto p. 415 **[checked]**, citing Ghoneim et al. 2012).
   - *Specialists:* Conyers (GPR) and Parcak (satellite archaeology) on the Giza claims: SAR "can't see through rock" (Retraction Watch, 31 August 2026 **[checked]**).
   - Depths of 40–100 m from satellite data (Merlin Burrows) cannot come from radar.

5. **An independent GPR survey found nothing Labyrinthine.**
   - Szynkiewicz (KART-GEO, Wrocław), working for Cairo University's Faculty of Archaeology, ran GPR to 20 m around the pyramid, with cores from 2002 and 2008. The aim was to trace groundwater inflow "and to verify possible links between the pyramid and a structure identified by some archaeologists as the labyrinth of Herodotus".
   - He found possible architectural remains on the east and south sides: "There is, however, nothing to support the idea that this was Herodotus's labyrinth." (*Archaeologia Polona* 53 (2015), 383–386, p. 386 **[checked]**.)

6. **The "under a pyramid" SAR method has lost its peer-reviewed footing.**
   - Biondi and Malanga's *Remote Sensing* paper of 2022, on Khufu's pyramid, the only refereed paper behind the Khafre Project, was retracted on 10 August 2026 for "serious methodological flaws and statistical errors" (Retraction Watch **[checked]**; the source records are proposed in `docs/research/ch8.md` as `biondi-malanga-2022` and `retraction-watch-2026-giza`).
   - Retraction Watch's article does not mention Hawara; the Hawara claims are in reader comments only (**[checked]**).
   - ARF itself says Stanford's Howard Zebker "will attempt to replicate" Biondi's results, which has not yet happened (ARF, "ArchaeoSAR", 2 May 2026 **[checked]**).

7. **Herodotus's lower storey is hearsay in his own account.** He says outright that he speaks of it from hearsay (II.148, **[checked]** as quoted by Carlotto p. 408). What the caretakers called royal and crocodile burials fits a pyramid substructure and animal cemeteries. Herodotus also credits the building to the Dodecarchy of the 7th century BCE, which nobody now accepts.

## 5. Where it stands

The building is real and identified. It is Amenemhat III's mortuary temple, demolished in antiquity down to its foundations and a thick bed of chips. Refereed geophysics by the very team behind Mataha shows shallow anomalies, at about 5–7.5 m, that could be walls and need digging. There is no published evidence for a deep, intact second storey, and none for Merlin Burrows' multi-level complex. Proponents are right on two points: the site has not been excavated at depth since 1911, and groundwater is now destroying what is there. Fieldwork reported to have begun in May 2026 is the first real test, and it has published nothing yet.

**Measured or interpreted.**

- *Measured:* resistivity, conductivity and radar backscatter values. Everything called a "closure", "wall-type structure" or "uncorrelated return" is an anomaly in the data.
- *Interpreted:* "empty volume (open cavity, shafts, halls, rooms)" (Elshazly p. 44), "walls and rooms of the labyrinth" (Khalil), "Labyrinth" (Carlotto's pin in Fig. 11), and everything in section C.
- *Renderings:*
  - Petrie's restored plan is an interpretation.
  - The Merlin Burrows images are colour-coded depth composites of an undisclosed method, effectively the author's rendering. Some are published "inverted".
  - Carlotto's polygons are the author's outlines.
  - No 3D model of the Labyrinth was found in a published paper. The 2023 preprint's "3D reconstruction … derived from video" is unseen.
