Seked

The strongest case for an older Giza, illustrated for an ordinary viewer: a scroll documentary in ten chapters. Every figure is computed from the measurement database, and the archaeologists get a reply in every chapter.

A steelman of Hancock, Bauval and Schoch, beautiful enough to show someone. The question moves from "does the claim hold?" to "what exactly is the claim, shown as well as it can be shown?".

Thesis

The claim in front, the numbers behind it

The September plan built a test bench: survey first, every claim an overlay with its residual, the app never saying true or false. That machinery stays, but it moves from the front of the piece to a supporting role. The piece now shows each claim of an older Giza as its proponents would want it shown. The numbers stand behind it, and the archaeologists' answer stands beside it.

Four rules carry the old plan's honesty into the new one.

  1. I

    The steelman is in the words and the pictures, never in the numbers. A claim is told at its strongest and drawn as well as it can be drawn. Its receipt still comes from data/, and when a claim misses its own tolerance (C2, C4 and C5 do), the receipt says so in the claim's own terms.

  2. II

    The reply is never omitted. Every chapter carries the archaeologists' strongest answer and the newest work, quieter than the claim and one tap away. Where the reply has a better picture, it gets that picture.

  3. III

    A claim state is labelled a claim. The First Time and the long rains are the proponents' reading, drawn as such. Nothing in them is entered as a measurement, and no evidence tier is promoted because a chapter needs it.

  4. IV

    The old rules hold. Nothing is typed twice. Drawings are read two ways. Stand-ins are labelled. Precession is Vondrák 2011.

Kept and scrapped

  • Kept: the renders and the five eras, the panoramas (as an optional "look around" inside a chapter), @seked/sky, the claims engine (now the receipts), the measurement database, and the render pipeline in render/.
  • Scrapped: the tour's stop-and-dissolve slideshow, apps/walk/tour.json and its player. They retire when the page replaces them (decision 6).
  • Rebuilt: the Sphinx, as one master mesh from which every later era is subtracted, and the First Time, with no pyramids.

Format

The page: a stage, a text, and the reader's scroll

Each chapter is a stage, a full-bleed picture pinned while its chapter scrolls, and a text, the narration as words in a column that scrolls over it. The text carries beats, points where the stage changes. Between two beats, the reader's scroll position drives one parameter of the stage: a frame, a crossfade, an epoch, or a line being drawn. The reader sets the pace, and nothing waits on a transition.

What every chapter has

  • The claim, told at full strength, in the narration's words.
  • The receipt, wherever there is a number: measured against claimed, the residual, the tolerance and the free choices the claim needed. It is computed live in the page from the bundled database, so switching a survey preset changes it, exactly as in the dossier.
  • The reply, a collapsed panel in a smaller, quieter type, holding the strongest mainstream answer, the newest work and its sources.
  • Look around, where a panorama exists for that station and era. It opens the walkthrough's panorama player in place, and closing it returns the reader to the same scroll position.

Autoplay

One button plays the narration and scrolls the page in time with it. Each beat is anchored to a word of the script, and the voice service's word timings turn those anchors into times. Any manual scroll pauses autoplay. Because the autoplayed page is a pure function of time, a capture script can step its clock frame by frame and record the trailer without dropped frames.

Build

A new app, apps/story, in Vite and plain TypeScript. There is no React: the page is a scroll timeline, a canvas and some SVG. It imports @seked/claims, @seked/sky and @seked/geometry, and reads seked.json from pnpm bundle as the viewer does. The script is one Markdown file per chapter with beat markers, so the page and the narration are built from the same text. It reuses the walkthrough's WebGL2 panorama player for "look around", and the viewer's WebCodecs recorder or a Playwright capture for the trailer.

Script

Ten chapters

The order follows the argument as its proponents build it: the stone first (it is older than it should be), then what it was and what faced it, then the texts that remember a First Time, then the sky that dates it, the numbers in the stone, the world before the flood that makes it thinkable, then what is said to lie underneath, and last, what would settle it. Chapter 8 was split in two on 20 October (decision 8): the underground claims are the most visual in the piece and need their own stage, and the closing chapter then answers every chapter, not just the cavities. The narration itself is drafted in docs/script.md once the research below is in.

Rebuild 1

The Sphinx: one master, subtracted

The lions disagree between eras because each is a separate Meshy generation (sphinx-lion-first, sphinx-lion-rain, sphinx-built, sphinx-meshy). In their place comes one master lion, the First Time's, with every later state derived from it by operations in Blender. The master is a stand-in fitted to the ARCE dimensions (sphinx.length, sphinx.height, sphinx.width), and nothing about its form is a measurement.

  1. I10,500 BCE

    The master, fresh cut

    The lion as the steelman has it, with crisp arrises and an unweathered ditch.

  2. II7000 BCE

    The long rains

    The same mesh, weathered. The softer beds recede faster than the harder ones, which rounds the profiles into coves, and vertical fissures open along the joints. The ditch walls take the same treatment. The beds follow the members of the Mokattam Formation that the body is cut through. Their heights and relative hardness are entered with sources where the literature gives them, and as stated look choices where it does not.

  3. III2500 BCE

    The king's head, inside the lion's

    A nemes head is carved inside the weathered lion head: a boolean that keeps only what lies inside both. The test is that the king's head must fit. If it does not at its natural size, it shrinks until it does, and the ratio is reported against today's head. That is the steelman's account of why the head is too small, as a number with its stand-in caveat.

  4. IVToday

    Further loss

    The nose and beard go, and the twentieth-century masonry repairs are laid on as different stone. As a check, the derived today is compared with sphinx-meshy, the mesh made from photographs, and its distance from it is reported.

The lion head ghosted round the king's head is a hero image in its own right, and it opens chapter 2. The operators live in render/giza/ as dependency-free Blender Python, one function per era, so the master can be swapped for a better one and every era follows.

Blocker. The Meshy meshes are in build/models/ on the laptop only. Both that and ~/.seked/models/ are gitignored, and neither is in the cloud container, so the operators can be written here but not run on the real master until it arrives (decision 1).

Rebuild 2

The First Time, without pyramids

As its proponents make the argument, the 10,500 BCE plateau holds the Sphinx, its temples and the plan, and no pyramids. The pyramids are built c. 2500 BCE to the older plan. Finished, gold-capped pyramids in a 10,500 BCE forest are the weakest form of the claim. The current First Time render shows exactly that, and Stewart's reading of 25 September in render/giza/states.py (pristine, gilded pyramids from an unknown people) is superseded by the brief.

  • What stays: the Sphinx (the master, fresh), the Sphinx Temple and the Valley Temple in megalithic mode, the green Sahara of the African Humid Period, the flood and the tropical sky. All of this exists.
  • What goes: the three pyramids and their caps, in both claim states (first-time and lion). In states.py, that is pyramids=None, with the pyramid, cap and gild code paths taught to honour it.
  • The plan: the bedrock where each pyramid will stand, including the natural knoll that the Great Pyramid's lower courses enclose. The three positions themselves are drawn by the page as an overlay (C4's belt on the ground), not built in Blender, so the render invents no monument (decision 2).

Chapter 8

Underneath: the known, the measured, the claimed and the legend

Chapter 8 shows what is said to lie under the plateau, and what is known to. Its stage is the plateau cut away on a north–south plane through Khafre's axis, the eastern half removed and the rock drawn as in an x-ray. The camera starts at the pyramid's base and goes down. The excavated rooms are a few metres under it. The claimed wells fall 648 m, and Khafre's pyramid is stacked beside them, four and a half times, for scale. Then the camera goes east to the Sphinx's paws, and south to Hawara, whose Labyrinth is then laid over Giza at the same scale. The research is in docs/research/khafre-sar.md and labyrinth.md, both checked by hand where the model rests on them.

Four layers, by tier

Every object in the chapter belongs to one tier in data/structures.json, and the tier sets its look: stone for excavated, a solid box with error bars for instrumented, a dashed outline for claimed, a ghost for legendary. No object changes tier because the chapter needs it (decision 27 of the night of 20 October).

  1. Iexcavated

    What has been walked into

    Khafre's substructure from data/measurements/g2-interior.json: the passages, the burial chamber with its floor at −3.0 m (Maragioglio and Rinaldi), and the lower chamber, 10.46 × 3.13 m (Petrie), whose level no record yet gives. It needs a new key from Hölscher's sections or Lehner. The Osiris Shaft, three levels to about 30 m under the causeway with water at the bottom (osiris-shaft), is the deepest excavated room on the plateau. At Hawara: Amenemhat III's pyramid and the Labyrinth's chip bed, as Petrie dug them.

  2. IIinstrumented

    What instruments found, with error bars

    The Great Pyramid's north-face corridor and Big Void from g1-voids.json, all verified: the corridor 9.06 ± 0.07 m long, the Void at least 30 m long with its centre 40 to 50 m above the Queen's Chamber floor. Alvarez's 1970 muon cone, drawn up from the Belzoni Chamber at its 35° half-angle, found no chamber in the 19 per cent of Khafre it saw. At Hawara: the shallow resistivity closures at 5 and 7.5 m that Elshazly and colleagues published in 2024, with the 2008 survey's leader among the authors. These are the model of what an answer looks like.

  3. IIIclaimed

    What has been announced

    The Khafre Project of March 2025 (g2.sar-shafts): five "Zeds" in the base, eight wells in two north–south rows wrapped in descending spirals, two cubes about 80 m on a side at −648 m, and structures to about 2 km. The cavity under the Sphinx's paws, about 5 m down (sphinx.paw-cavity). At Hawara: the Mataha walls at 8–12 m (2008, unpublished), and the Merlin Burrows levels to about 100 m (2015, published by De Cordier in 2025). None of it is in a journal.

  4. IVlegendary

    What is told

    The Hall of Records under the paw (hall-of-records, from Cayce), and Herodotus's lower storey at Hawara: 1,500 rooms he was not shown, which he reports from hearsay.

The claimants' own pictures

The chapter shows what the claimants published beside what we drew from it, so a viewer can check that we drew what they said.

  • Tomograms and slices. The Khafre Project's tomograms (its abstract of 16 March 2025, pp. 4–6) have no axes and no scale bar. They are hung as plates on the section plane, fitted by their own labels (the base, 648 m), and captioned "placed by its labels, not scaled". Nothing is scaled from them.
  • Figures with axes. Elshazly's ERT sections, Szynkiewicz's radargrams and Carlotto's Fig. 11 (with its 144 m bar) are registered with scripts/plate.py and read two ways: a printed dimension is transcribed, and a scaled distance is scaled from plate, with its sigma.
  • Rights. The retracted 2022 Khufu paper is CC BY 4.0, so its figures are hosted with "retracted 10 August 2026" beside them. Petrie's and Lepsius's plates are in the public domain. Everything else is linked, not hosted, until permission is given. The requests are drafted in docs/permissions/ for Stewart to send (decision 10).

The model of the claim

The claimed underground is built procedurally from the claimants' numbers: a dependency-free geometry module in render/giza/, tested in render/tests/, and a Blender builder for the cutaway. It is never made by Meshy or an image model, which would add detail nobody has claimed. Every figure is a record in data/measurements/g2-sar.json with method: "as claimed", citing the slide, paper or video it comes from, and verified: false until it has been checked there.

  • From their numbers: the count of wells, the two rows, the depth of 648 m, the cubes' 80 m, the five Zeds and the 2 km.
  • Stated look choices: the wells' diameter (10–12 m, from the press, not the abstract), the rows' spacing and offset (taken from the authors' own 3D rendering, inside the footprint about its centre), the spirals' pitch and the Zeds' size. None of these is published. Each is labelled where it is drawn and lives in one table in the geometry module.

The Labyrinth at Hawara, and at Giza for scale

Hawara is a site of its own in data/sites.json, with its own frame: origin at the pyramid's base centre, +X east, +Y north. Petrie's figures go in as records: the chip bed "about 1000 feet long, and 800 feet broad" (1889, p. 5), the pyramid's brick base of 3692 in, the presumptive casing of 157 in and the casing angle of 48° 45′ (1890, p. 13). The finished base and the height are computed, as Petrie computed them. Which way the chip bed's long side runs is read from his Pl. XXV, two ways, or is a stated choice.

  • The still: the chip bed and the pyramid laid beside the Great Pyramid at the same scale, labelled "at Hawara, 80 km south; laid here for scale". The chip bed is about 1.4 times Khufu's base, and the pyramid a little smaller than Menkaure's.
  • The section: north–south, south of the pyramid. Petrie's chips (0.6 to 7.3 m), Elshazly's closures, the Mataha walls dashed and labelled "reported, unpublished", the water table from 1.5 to 10 m, limestone at 12 to 15 m, and Herodotus's lower storey as a ghost. Whatever is down there is drowning.

The receipts

  • Depths against heights. 648 m against g2.height.original is four and a half Khafres. Against the burial chamber's floor at −3.0 m, the claimed wells go about 216 times deeper than any known room of Khafre's.
  • The known against the claimed. One depth scale for every object in the chapter, coloured by tier.
  • The Big Void's sigma. An instrumented answer gives a position with error bars. The claimed shafts and the paw cavity give none, and the receipt says so.
  • The Labyrinth's area against Khufu's base, computed by @seked/geometry from Petrie's records.
  • Three lines in the section: what radar is measured to image in dry sand (about 1.5 m; up to 10 m by ESA's figure), the water table (sharafeldin-2019, once Khufu's base elevation above sea level is a record), and the claim's 648 m.

The reply

The only peer-reviewed paper behind the method, Biondi and Malanga's 2022 work on Khufu, was retracted on 10 August 2026 for "serious methodological flaws and statistical errors"; the authors disagree. Satellite radar sees the surface, and Biondi agrees that it reaches less than 10 m into rock, so the dispute is whether surface vibrations can be measured from orbit and inverted to 648 m. No data or code has been released, and nobody has reprocessed it. Egypt's antiquities authority permitted no work at Khafre, and Hawass says the base is bedrock carved to about 8 m. Deep shafts would sit hundreds of metres below the water table. At Hawara, Petrie's finds name Amenemhat III. The 2008 survey's own leader published only shallow closures in 2024, and an independent GPR survey found "nothing to support the idea that this was Herodotus's labyrinth". The reply's picture is the claim's own section with the three lines drawn in, and the empty column between 10 m and 648 m.

Sound

Narration, autoplay and the trailer

  • The script is about 150–220 spoken words per chapter, roughly a minute to a minute and a half each, so twelve to fifteen minutes in all. Each chapter's beats are anchored to its words. A reader who never presses play reads the same words.
  • The voice comes from ElevenLabs. Three candidates read the cold open, and Stewart picks one (decision 5). Its timestamped output gives each beat its time.
  • The reply in autoplay is one sentence per chapter, in a second, quieter voice, so that "never omitted" holds for a viewer who only watches (decision 3).
  • The trailer is 60 to 90 seconds cut from the autoplay. A Playwright script steps the page's clock, captures each frame and muxes them with the audio. It runs in the cloud, where Chromium is installed.

Work

Who does what

  • The cloud thinks. It writes the plan and the script, reviews the research, writes the code (apps/story, the Sphinx operators, the First Time) and enters data. It makes low-sample Cycles previews: Blender 5.1.2 runs headless here on four CPUs, and EEVEE does not.
  • The laptop renders. A local session pulls the branch and runs the final Cycles renders and sequences on the RTX 5070 Ti through scripts/job.py, then deploys. Git is the hand-off, and one session owns the repo at a time.
  • Headless jobs do the bulk research and drafting, on the API key, through scripts/headless.py. Each chapter's research is python scripts/research.py start N, which writes docs/research/chN.md. The script's first draft is one such job at extra effort, which the cloud then edits.
  • Effort. Planning and the script run at extra effort, building at high. Max is only for a named, narrow hard problem, and that is said first.

Risks

The hard parts, named

  • Reading as advocacy, or as a debunk. Three of the headline claims miss their own tolerance. The narration tells each claim at its strongest, and the receipt states the miss plainly. Rule I is the guard. The reply panel is the release valve.
  • Sources from memory. The first headless pilot had no web access and wrote every citation from memory. Research jobs now mark each citation checked or unchecked, and nothing reaches data/ until it has been checked against the page.
  • Weight. Ten chapters of frame sequences could reach 100 MB. Each chapter loads one ahead, phones get 1200-pixel frames, and the budget is about 12 MB per chapter on a desktop.
  • Render time. About 600–900 final frames at one to two minutes each on the laptop is 10–30 hours, spread over several nights through job.py.
  • The master Sphinx is a stand-in. The head-fit number is read off a Meshy mesh scaled to the ARCE dimensions, and its receipt says so. A better master changes the number.
  • Göbekli Tepe has no 3D source. A stand-in is built from published plans (read two ways, like any drawing) and CC photographs, and labelled. If that cannot be made honest, the chapter uses photographs instead.
  • Copyright. Quotations stay short. Plates of stelae and texts come from licensed photographs, with attribution.
  • Lending weight by scale. A 648 m shaft drawn beautifully under a pyramid is more persuasive than the press conference it came from. The claimed tier's look (dashed, never stone), the label on every look choice and the reply's empty column are the guard. The claim is drawn as large as it is said, no larger.
  • Rights to the underground pictures. The Khafre Project's tomograms, the Merlin Burrows images and the Mataha figures belong to their authors. Until they answer, the chapter links them and draws its own model, and the comparison that lets a viewer check our drawing waits.
  • Hawara is not Giza. The Labyrinth is about 80 km south and some 700 years after Khufu, and even its proponents do not say it dates Giza. It is in the chapter because the claims are made together and because "it would have been found by now" is the argument it tests. Its overlay on Giza is labelled as laid there for scale.

Sources

Research and sources

Each chapter's research is a headless job whose brief lands in docs/research/chN.md. It gives the claim at full strength, the proponents' evidence, the receipts, the reply, the state of the question, the pictures, the open questions, and the new sources in data/sources.json's shape. Its citations are marked checked or unchecked. The briefs are drafts, and nothing in them is data until it is entered in data/.

All eight briefs came back on 20 October, about 4,300 to 5,900 words each, for $16.22 on the API key: a dollar and a half to two and a half a chapter, 48 to 72 turns each. The tallies below count the marks in each brief and say nothing yet about which citations matter most.
