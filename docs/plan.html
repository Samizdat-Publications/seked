Thesis

The claim in front, the numbers behind it

The September plan built a test bench: survey first, every claim an overlay with its residual, the app never saying true or false. That machinery stays, but it moves from the front of the piece to a supporting role. The piece now shows each claim of an older Giza as its proponents would want it shown. The numbers stand behind it, and the archaeologists' answer stands beside it.

Four rules carry the old plan's honesty into the new one.

  1. I

    The steelman is in the words and the pictures, never in the numbers. A claim is told at its strongest and drawn as well as it can be drawn. Its receipt still comes from data/, and when a claim misses its own tolerance (C2, C4 and C5 do), the receipt says so in the claim's own terms.

  2. II

    The reply is never omitted. Every chapter carries the archaeologists' strongest answer and the newest work, quieter than the claim and one tap away. Where the reply has a better picture, it gets that picture.

  3. III

    A claim state is labelled a claim. The First Time and the long rains are the proponents' reading, drawn as such. Nothing in them is entered as a measurement, and no evidence tier is promoted because a chapter needs it.

  4. IV

    The old rules hold. Nothing is typed twice. Drawings are read two ways. Stand-ins are labelled. Precession is Vondrák 2011.

Kept and scrapped

  • Kept: the renders and the five eras, the panoramas (as an optional "look around" inside a chapter), @seked/sky, the claims engine (now the receipts), the measurement database, and the render pipeline in render/.
  • Scrapped: the tour's stop-and-dissolve slideshow, apps/walk/tour.json and its player. They retire when the page replaces them (decision 6).
  • Rebuilt: the Sphinx, as one master mesh from which every later era is subtracted, and the First Time, with no pyramids.

Format

The page: a stage, a text, and the reader's scroll

Each chapter is a stage, a full-bleed picture pinned while its chapter scrolls, and a text, the narration as words in a column that scrolls over it. The text carries beats, points where the stage changes. Between two beats, the reader's scroll position drives one parameter of the stage: a frame, a crossfade, an epoch, or a line being drawn. The reader sets the pace, and nothing waits on a transition.

What every chapter has

  • The claim, told at full strength, in the narration's words.
  • The receipt, wherever there is a number: measured against claimed, the residual, the tolerance and the free choices the claim needed. It is computed live in the page from the bundled database, so switching a survey preset changes it, exactly as in the dossier.
  • The reply, a collapsed panel in a smaller, quieter type, holding the strongest mainstream answer, the newest work and its sources.
  • Look around, where a panorama exists for that station and era. It opens the walkthrough's panorama player in place, and closing it returns the reader to the same scroll position.

Autoplay

One button plays the narration and scrolls the page in time with it. Each beat is anchored to a word of the script, and the voice service's word timings turn those anchors into times. Any manual scroll pauses autoplay. Because the autoplayed page is a pure function of time, a capture script can step its clock frame by frame and record the trailer without dropped frames.

Build

A new app, apps/story, in Vite and plain TypeScript. There is no React: the page is a scroll timeline, a canvas and some SVG. It imports @seked/claims, @seked/sky and @seked/geometry, and reads seked.json from pnpm bundle as the viewer does. The script is one Markdown file per chapter with beat markers, so the page and the narration are built from the same text. It reuses the walkthrough's WebGL2 panorama player for "look around", and the viewer's WebCodecs recorder or a Playwright capture for the trailer.

Rebuild 1

The Sphinx: one master, subtracted

The lions disagree between eras because each is a separate Meshy generation (sphinx-lion-first, sphinx-lion-rain, sphinx-built, sphinx-meshy). In their place comes one master lion, the First Time's, with every later state derived from it by operations in Blender. The master is a stand-in fitted to the ARCE dimensions (sphinx.length, sphinx.height, sphinx.width), and nothing about its form is a measurement.

  1. I10,500 BCE

    The master, fresh cut

    The lion as the steelman has it, with crisp arrises and an unweathered ditch.

  2. II7000 BCE

    The long rains

    The same mesh, weathered. The softer beds recede faster than the harder ones, which rounds the profiles into coves, and vertical fissures open along the joints. The ditch walls take the same treatment. The beds follow the members of the Mokattam Formation that the body is cut through. Their heights and relative hardness are entered with sources where the literature gives them, and as stated look choices where it does not.

  3. III2500 BCE

    The king's head, inside the lion's

    A nemes head is carved inside the weathered lion head: a boolean that keeps only what lies inside both. The test is that the king's head must fit. If it does not at its natural size, it shrinks until it does, and the ratio is reported against today's head. That is the steelman's account of why the head is too small, as a number with its stand-in caveat.

  4. IVToday

    Further loss

    The nose and beard go, and the twentieth-century masonry repairs are laid on as different stone. As a check, the derived today is compared with sphinx-meshy, the mesh made from photographs, and its distance from it is reported.

The lion head ghosted round the king's head is a hero image in its own right, and it opens chapter 2. The operators live in render/giza/ as dependency-free Blender Python, one function per era, so the master can be swapped for a better one and every era follows.

Blocker. The Meshy meshes are in build/models/ on the laptop only. Both that and ~/.seked/models/ are gitignored, and neither is in the cloud container, so the operators can be written here but not run on the real master until it arrives (decision 1).

Rebuild 2

The First Time, without pyramids

As its proponents make the argument, the 10,500 BCE plateau holds the Sphinx, its temples and the plan, and no pyramids. The pyramids are built c. 2500 BCE to the older plan. Finished, gold-capped pyramids in a 10,500 BCE forest are the weakest form of the claim. The current First Time render shows exactly that, and Stewart's reading of 25 September in render/giza/states.py (pristine, gilded pyramids from an unknown people) is superseded by the brief.

  • What stays: the Sphinx (the master, fresh), the Sphinx Temple and the Valley Temple in megalithic mode, the green Sahara of the African Humid Period, the flood and the tropical sky. All of this exists.
  • What goes: the three pyramids and their caps, in both claim states (first-time and lion). In states.py, that is pyramids=None, with the pyramid, cap and gild code paths taught to honour it.
  • The plan: the bedrock where each pyramid will stand, including the natural knoll that the Great Pyramid's lower courses enclose. The three positions themselves are drawn by the page as an overlay (C4's belt on the ground), not built in Blender, so the render invents no monument (decision 2).

Sound

Narration, autoplay and the trailer

  • The script is about 150–220 spoken words per chapter, roughly a minute to a minute and a half each, so twelve to fifteen minutes in all. Each chapter's beats are anchored to its words. A reader who never presses play reads the same words.
  • The voice comes from ElevenLabs. Three candidates read the cold open, and Stewart picks one (decision 5). Its timestamped output gives each beat its time.
  • The reply in autoplay is one sentence per chapter, in a second, quieter voice, so that "never omitted" holds for a viewer who only watches (decision 3).
  • The trailer is 60 to 90 seconds cut from the autoplay. A Playwright script steps the page's clock, captures each frame and muxes them with the audio. It runs in the cloud, where Chromium is installed.

Work

Who does what

  • The cloud thinks. It writes the plan and the script, reviews the research, writes the code (apps/story, the Sphinx operators, the First Time) and enters data. It makes low-sample Cycles previews: Blender 5.1.2 runs headless here on four CPUs, and EEVEE does not.
  • The laptop renders. A local session pulls the branch and runs the final Cycles renders and sequences on the RTX 5070 Ti through scripts/job.py, then deploys. Git is the hand-off, and one session owns the repo at a time.
  • Headless jobs do the bulk research and drafting, on the API key, through scripts/headless.py. Each chapter's research is python scripts/research.py start N, which writes docs/research/chN.md. The script's first draft is one such job at extra effort, which the cloud then edits.
  • Effort. Planning and the script run at extra effort, building at high. Max is only for a named, narrow hard problem, and that is said first.

Risks

The hard parts, named

  • Reading as advocacy, or as a debunk. Three of the headline claims miss their own tolerance. The narration tells each claim at its strongest, and the receipt states the miss plainly. Rule I is the guard. The reply panel is the release valve.
  • Sources from memory. The first headless pilot had no web access and wrote every citation from memory. Research jobs now mark each citation checked or unchecked, and nothing reaches data/ until it has been checked against the page.
  • Weight. Nine chapters of frame sequences could reach 100 MB. Each chapter loads one ahead, phones get 1200-pixel frames, and the budget is about 12 MB per chapter on a desktop.
  • Render time. About 600–900 final frames at one to two minutes each on the laptop is 10–30 hours, spread over several nights through job.py.
  • The master Sphinx is a stand-in. The head-fit number is read off a Meshy mesh scaled to the ARCE dimensions, and its receipt says so. A better master changes the number.
  • Göbekli Tepe has no 3D source. A stand-in is built from published plans (read two ways, like any drawing) and CC photographs, and labelled. If that cannot be made honest, the chapter uses photographs instead.
  • Copyright. Quotations stay short. Plates of stelae and texts come from licensed photographs, with attribution.

For Stewart

Decisions

  1. 1

    How the laptop's assets reach the cloud. The master Sphinx is one need. The other is any preview of a whole era: the first attempt here (20 October) failed for want of the Meshy GLBs and of the Sketchfab props that the plant library builds from, the date palm among them. The Poly Haven textures fetch here without a key. There are two routes. The laptop could commit what it holds through Git LFS (.gitignore already expects LFS for *.glb), or the cloud environment could be given MESHY_API_KEY and SKETCHFAB_TOKEN as secrets, so that scripts/models.py, scripts/props.py and scripts/meshy.py fetch them as the laptop does.

    Recommendation: the two secrets. The fetch scripts already pin every file by its checksum, so the cloud gets the same bytes. LFS would version large binaries that the manifests can already reproduce.

  2. 2

    Recommendation: knolls and the overlay. Built markers would be a monument nobody claims.

  5. 5

Sources

Research and sources

Each chapter's research is a headless job whose brief lands in docs/research/chN.md. It gives the claim at full strength, the proponents' evidence, the receipts, the reply, the state of the question, the pictures, the open questions, and the new sources in data/sources.json's shape. Its citations are marked checked or unchecked. The briefs are drafts, and nothing in them is data until it is entered in data/.

Research status: filled in as each chapter's brief comes back.
