## Decisions, with the reason for each

1. **Headless jobs do not use `--bare`.** It cuts the tools to Bash, Edit and Read, so the first pilot
   had no web access. Auth stays on the key: the key in the environment outranks any login in print
   mode, and the job's config directory holds none. A bogus key is refused.
2. **Research runs as one job per chapter**, Opus at high effort, $4 and 120 turns a chapter. The
   pilot cost $2.12 and took 72 turns; the eight cost $16.22.
3. **The September plan is kept** as `docs/plan-2026-09.html`. `docs/plan.html` is the rethink, so the
   links that already point at the plan now show the current one.
4. **No pyramid before 2500 BCE**, in the renders and in the viewer. The sites are flattened, as
   before, so GLO-30's mounds do not come back.
5. **Plants grow over the empty sites.** The first preview showed the flattened squares as bare,
   hard-edged clearings in the forest, which reads as a monument nobody claims. The page draws the plan
   (plan decision 2); the render draws ground.
6. **The cloud's Sketchfab secret is read by its own name** (`SKETCHFAB_API_KEY` as well as
   `SKETCHFAB_TOKEN`), rather than renaming the secret.
7. **The first master attempt was not meshed.** Four Commons photographs restyled one at a time gave
   four different statues: three short, tall-chested statuettes with naturalistic faces and a rope
   tail, one with the real body but still weathered (`docs/master-lion-restyle-2026-10-20.jpg`).
   36 credits spent; the mesh's 35 saved.
8. **The second attempt starts from one photograph**: "Giza Plateau - Great Sphinx - side view.JPG"
   (CC BY-SA 4.0), a full-length south profile, because a single side view holds the body's real length
   and every other view is then drawn from it.
9. **The briefs' new sources are not entered wholesale** (about 180, with overlaps). They go into
   `data/sources.json` as the script uses them, each `verified: false`.
10. **Commons requests use Wikimedia's standard thumbnail widths** (1920, not 2048). Since January
    2026 any other width is throttled hard, and from this container's shared address the throttle
    asked for 600 s pauses until it refused outright. `meshy.py` snaps every request down to a standard
    width, and a long `Retry-After` falls back to the original rather than stalling.
11. **The master is drawn as named views in the manifest** (`views`, `mesh_from` in
    `blender/models.json`), drawn by `meshy.py images ID --view NAME`, and kept with their prompts and
    references in `assets/meshy/refs/sphinx-master/`. The record of what the mesh was built from lives
    in the repository, not on one machine.
12. **A redesign prompt does not hold the body; a local edit might.** The side view was tried twice:
    from the photograph alone (9 credits), and then from the photograph plus that drawing as the head
    reference (9 credits). Both times the model drew a compact park lion in three-quarter view with its
    tail curled in the air, and the second time it copied the head reference outright. The head itself
    was right: flat planes, a squared muzzle, and a mane falling in lappets that prefigure the nemes. So
    the third try is two local edits of the photograph: first change only the head, then only the
    surface. The fallback, if the body still shrinks, is to stretch the mesh in Blender between the
    haunch and the chest, which is how the current lion was lengthened, though that was done in 2D.
13. **The local edits worked.** The south profile with only its head swapped kept the monument's own
    long, low body and camera. Dressing only its surface then gave a clean fresh-cut lion. The front and
    back photographs were swapped and dressed the same way, with the side's swap as the head's reference,
    so the head is the same in every view. Twice the dressing step invented a tail looping in the air
    where the photograph had repair masonry. A third edit, of the tail only, laid it flat on the floor
    round the haunch, as on the real Sphinx. All three views agree (`assets/meshy/refs/sphinx-master/`).
    Credits: 126 on images, then 35 for the mesh.
14. **The eras move the master's own vertices.** No voxel remesh: the master is welded at its UV seams
    and subdivided once, so its texture survives and the render can lay the enclosure's bedrock over it,
    as it does for the other lions.
15. **The beds: Member I's thickness is sourced (Lehner 1994), the seven alternating Member II beds are
    sourced (AERA), and the rest are look choices** (`render/giza/weathering.py`). Member III's base sits
    at 0.62 of the height, and the rates and depths are stated, until an ARCE plate is scaled for the
    contacts.
