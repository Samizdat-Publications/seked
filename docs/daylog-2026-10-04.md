## Done

1. **Detail patches for the tour's close views.** The intro (24 degree field) and the akhet stop (50)
   enlarged the 4096-wide panorama two to five times on a 1080 or 1440-line screen and read soft. Each
   such stop now has, per era, the same station rendered through a lens (render/patches.py, 11 patches),
   which the walkthrough lays over the panorama with feathered edges. A patch older than its panorama is
   left out by publish.py, so re-rendering a station means re-rendering its patches
   (`python render/patches.py`, then the queue).
2. **A fetch loop in the walkthrough.** Prefetch fetched files it never marked as used, so past twelve
   kept files the newest was dropped and fetched again at once (229 fetches in 15 s at the akhet stop).
3. **The King's Chamber's granite.** It read as an orange camouflage print in an empty box. The
   crystals are finer with broken outlines, blended softer; the faces polished (they catch the
   lamps); each block varies more; today's walls are darker and glossier at hand height and scuffed
   along the floor, the stone a greyer rose. The doorway is cut through with 2.4 m of passage behind
   it instead of a flat dark panel. All three eras re-rendered.
4. **The megalithic temples.** At the lost-chapter stop they read as tiled soap bars. Courses and
   block lengths now vary (lognormal, as Khafre's granite already did) and the fresh limestone has
   more grain and relief. Every First Time and long-rains outdoor station re-rendered with its patches.
5. **The built casing.** Through the earth stop's new patch the c. 2500 BCE casing read as rows of
   pebbles: each stone ringed with grime from an occlusion reaching 1.5 m, and a dark crescent under
   each from its 1 cm rounded arris in the low sun. The grime is now a hairline (0.25 m, 0.3) and the
   arris 4 mm. Every outdoor built station re-rendered with its patches.
